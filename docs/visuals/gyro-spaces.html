Local, World and Player gyro spaces

Turn the phone on the left (held in landscape, as when gaming) with two sets of sliders: Each slider turns the phone from where it is now: Local around its own axes as they point now, World around real-world axes (gravity, the long side laid level, the short side laid level). It shows the phone's own axes. The right view is the game camera the gyro steers in the chosen space: it turns and looks up and down but never rolls. Local reads turns about the phone's own axes; World about gravity, the long side laid level and the short side laid level; Player about a yaw axis made from gravity, with the phone's own pitch and roll. Every turn is split into yaw, pitch and roll along those axes (bars), and the game cameras below show what a gyro mouse setup (by default yaw → camera yaw, pitch → camera pitch; change it under Mapping) does with it.

World vs Player: the lines they turn around

Both spaces turn yaw around an "up" line. World uses true up, gravity. Player takes gravity and drops the part that runs along the phone's long side (the dashed line), so its up always stands square to that side, between the phone's own yaw and roll axes. So Player's up ignores how far one end of the phone dips. Then it boosts the turn ×1.41, but never past the phone's own yaw and roll together. For pitch, World uses the long side laid level, while Player uses the long side as it is, so when one end dips, Player's pitch line dips with it.

How much yaw and pitch each space reads from one full degree of each turn, with the phone held as now. Flat or only tilted back, the two lines are the same and the spaces differ only by the boost. Dip one end and they part: World follows gravity, Player stays square to the phone's long side.

Game camera in each space

Like a shooter's camera: yaw turns it, pitch looks up and down (stopping at straight up or down), and it never rolls, so roll from the phone is simply lost here. One phone degree turns the camera one degree. Level cameras points every camera level and ahead from wherever the phone is now, as if you started steering from this hold.

How each space uses gravity

Local ignores gravity: yaw, pitch and roll are the phone's own three turns, so turning a tilted phone leaks into roll.

World rebuilds yaw, pitch and roll around real-world lines: yaw is only the part of the turn around gravity, pitch around the phone's long side laid level, roll around its short side laid level.

Player changes yaw only. Its yaw axis is gravity with the part along the phone's pitch axis removed, so it always lies between the phone's yaw and roll axes: phone yaw and phone roll both count as turning, weighted by how gravity leans between them, eased ×1.41 but never more than both together. Pitch and roll stay the phone's own. It matches World while gravity has no part along the pitch axis (only pitch changed from flat); they differ once one end of the phone dips.
