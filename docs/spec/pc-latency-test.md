# Latency test

Two ways to see how long input takes to reach a game, both on the companion: **Measure buttons** on Home's latency card (always on while switched on, no video), and the **Latency test** page, which times the gyro from a slow-motion video. Both need the Wi-Fi or USB link; Bluetooth HID has no companion to measure with.

## Measure buttons

A switch on Home's **Latency** card (off by default, saved as `MeasureButtons` in the settings). While it is on, a line under the graph shows **Buttons: X ms, phone part Y ms** (the middle of the last 64 presses and releases, how many, and the newest one).

- It times each press or release from the moment the phone sees the input (the touch event's or controller key event's own time) to the moment the PC hands the STATE carrying it to the game (SendInput and the virtual pad). The **phone part** is from the input to the phone sending that STATE: Android's input delivery, the app, and any wait a gesture makes on purpose (a When with a Held chip waits up to the Tap time to tell a tap from a hold; a tap's output comes when the finger lifts).
- It leaves out what happens before the phone sees the input (the touchscreen's or controller's own delay) and after the game gets it (the game and the monitor).
- How: the PC sends TEST_MODE with PRESS_TIMES. The phone notes the newest touch down or up, controller key edge and mouse button edge (`PressStamp`, forgotten after 1 s if nothing changed) and, after each STATE that first carries a new press or release, sends PRESS_TIME: that STATE's time and the input's time, both in the phone's clock. The PC notes when it injected each STATE (`PressLatency`), matches the PRESS_TIME to the first injection that carried its STATE (a burst injects only its newest STATE), and turns the input time into PC time with the clock offset from LINK_INFO, as the link latency does. Off, the phone sends nothing extra.

## Latency test page

A sidebar page in the companion's main window, for anyone (not a debug tool). It measures the time from turning the phone to the game seeing the mouse move, including the screen, from a video filmed with a second phone.

1. **Start test** opens the **test window**: black, with one tall green bar that moves left and right only by the mouse movement GyroPhone injects (Windows raw input with no device, which is what SendInput makes); a real mouse is ignored. While the phone twists the bar follows the mouse exactly (1:1, a fixed middle and scale): any re-centring or squeezing would shift the bar's motion in time and make the result read low. The middle and scale are set again only between takes, when the mouse has rested for 0.4 s (moved under 5% of a swing): the bar jumps back to the middle and the next take's scale fits the last take's biggest swing to 40% of the width. Where the bar would leave the window it is hidden instead of held at the edge, so those frames are left out; the hint says to rest a moment when it disappears. Esc or Stop test closes it. While it is open the phone shows its **markers** (below), without anything to do on the phone.
2. The person holds the phone in front of the test window, screen toward them, and twists the wrist left and right for about 10 seconds. The workspace's gyro must move the mouse; nothing else changes.
3. They film it in slow motion (240 fps or more) with another phone from behind the hand, along the forearm, so the twist shows as the phone tilting in the picture like a steering wheel, markers and green bar in the picture, and copy the original video file to the PC (shared copies are often slowed to 30 fps).
4. They drop the video on the page (or Choose video…). The analyser reads it and adds a result. The video needs no trimming: walking up and putting the phone down are left out by themselves (below). The line under the drop area shows the progress, then the result or why there is none; it stays when the window is closed and opened again, and each outcome is also written to the companion's log. The video is first copied to `last-video.<ext>` in `Documents\GyroPhone\Latency` (replacing the one before), so the last video tried can always be found again.

**Phone markers** (`LatencyMarkers`): while TEST_MODE has LATENCY_SCREEN, the pad shows a black screen with four ArUco markers (4×4 dictionary, ids 0 to 3) in a 16-cell grid in the top half (the camera end, in every rotation: the screen is drawn in the phone's own portrait), clear of the hand holding the phone from below, and the line "Twist your wrist left and right". Each marker is a one-cell quiet zone around its 6 × 6 black square, the squares 8 cells apart, drawn on whole pixels in light grey rather than white (a white screen glows on camera into the markers' thin black border). It takes no touches, so input keeps working; **Hide markers** hides it until the next test. While the markers show, the pad never goes black (Black screen) and the screen stays on.

**Analyser** (`GyroPhone.Latency`, OpenCV through OpenCvSharp): per frame (frames decoded in order on the CPU (the graphics card's decoder was slower and lost frames), then looked at in batches over all cores, at 640 pixels on the short side) it finds the markers and reads the phone's **tilt** within the picture (like a steering wheel; from the direction of the markers' top and bottom edges, one marker is enough). Only this one reading of the phone is used: others (its left-right position, its turn toward the camera) also pick up the hand sliding and swinging, and on one video gave answers 8 ms apart, so picking whichever matched better only hid that. It takes the green bar's left-right centre in a band of rows just above the top of the phone's marker grid (from the perspective between the grid's known layout and the markers seen), so both are read from nearly the same lines of the picture (a phone camera captures lines one after another); a frame where the bar touches the picture's edge (cut off, so its centre is wrong) counts as not found. Only the **twisting stretch** is timed: the video is cut into half-second pieces, and the longest run of pieces where both the phone and the bar are mostly tracked and the phone turns at least a quarter as fast as in its brisk pieces (one quiet piece inside is allowed) is kept; the page says which part it timed when that isn't the whole video. Both motions are filled where tracking missed, freed of slow drift (0.6 s moving average) and scaled alike. The whole stretch's best lag (0 to 250 ms) checks that they match at all (under 0.5 the page says why instead of giving a number) and gives the direction (it follows the mouse mapping). Then each **swing** (half-twist: from one crossing of the middle to the next, wobble under a fifth of the motion's size ignored) is timed on its own: the lag, to a fraction of a frame, where the bar best repeats that swing, scored by that swing's own correlation. Swings matching under 0.8, or with more than 5% of frames out of sight or frozen, are left out. **Freezes** are the bar standing still for 0.1 s or more while the phone moves (the motion stalls, then catches up); they are left out of the latency and reported on their own line, with the longest, since a player feels them. The **latency** is the middle (median) of the swings' lags, and the **margin** is how far that middle could be off (95%: the swings' spread, from their middle half, over the root of their count). With fewer than 3 swings timed, the latency is the whole stretch's lag over the clean frames and the result is marked **unreliable**. Frame times come from the video, so its real frame rate is used; under 100 fps the page says why instead of giving a number.

**Results**: each one is saved as JSON in `Documents\GyroPhone\Latency` (`LatencyResults` and `LatencyRun` live in `GyroPhone.Latency`, so Claude's own runs of the analyser on the PC save there too, tagged `claude`: the list and the card show the tag, and the page picks up new files as they land) and listed newest first with **Open** (the card in a window). After every saved result, `results.html` in the same folder is rewritten from all of them (`LatencyReport`): one self-contained web page, newest first, with a table (date, latency ± margin, twists, match, freezes, swings left out, video, fps, link, phone, monitor, app, tag) and one second of both motions per run as a small chart. Unreliable runs are marked. Older results (from before the swing-by-swing analyser) used an older analyser (a different spread and stretches instead of swings) and a test window that re-centred the bar, which read about 5 to 7 ms low. The Results card's **Open results** button rewrites the page and opens it in the default browser; there is no image or data export, the page is the export. The card (1000 × 520, fixed dark colours) shows the latency, twists and margin (or unreliable), the freezes and swings left out, the link, phone, monitor refresh rate (of the test window's screen), video frame rate, companion version and date, and one second of both motions on top of each other (the second that matches best at the measured latency, so a typical one).

What the number includes: the phone's gyro and app, the link, the companion, Windows' desktop compositor drawing the test window, and the monitor. Rolling shutter can still leave about a millisecond or two when the camera reads the picture across its width.
