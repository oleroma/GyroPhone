# Phone: pad screen and edit mode

What the person sees and touches: the pad, its top row, the ← / → corners, edit mode and the mapping editor. What touches do is in [Mapping system](phone-mapping.md); settings pages are in [Settings](phone-settings.md).

## The app is the pad

`ui/MainActivity.kt` shows `ui/PadMenu.kt` `PadTab`: the pad (`ui/PadScreen.kt`) fills the screen, and every other page (Controller, Settings, Connection, a workspace's settings) opens in its place. Back steps out one page at a time. There are no other tabs.

**Play mode** draws the active workspace's controls in its placement for the current orientation (landscape and portrait placements are kept apart; a control with no portrait placement gets one derived from landscape, squeezed into the lower part with the top left to the touchpad). Empty space is the workspace touchpad. Each finger on a stick owns it until it lifts; a finger on a button goes to the engine; overlapping controls are allowed (front one wins unless a control behind has "shares touches"; order by size, or Front/Back). Play mode asks Android for unbuffered touch and joystick input (`TouchStream`) and passes the event time on, so the engine sees touches at their real time. A system cancel (an OxygenOS three-finger gesture taking the pointers) is handled phone-side: every finger is let go cleanly.

## Top row (capsules)

`ui/CapsuleRow.kt`, shown in play mode when not in fullscreen:

- **+** adds a workspace at the front (Blank, Windows touchpad or a preset, see [Workspaces](phone-workspaces.md)).
- One capsule per **workspace**: tap to switch; tap the current one to open its edit mode.
- **Controller** (the device screens, [Controllers](phone-controllers.md)), **App** (gear: the whole app's settings), **Defaults** (sliders: what every workspace follows, [Settings](phone-settings.md)), **Connection** (shows the link state).

The row scrolls. Long-press a capsule and drag it to reorder; moving workspaces reorders them. The order is stored as a string of `W`/`D`/`S` (App)/`F` (Defaults)/`C`; an order saved before Defaults existed gets it right after App.

## ← / → corners and fullscreen

The status and navigation bars never show in the app, on the pad, in settings or in any dialog (`ui/AppDialogs.kt`: the app's `Dialog` and `AlertDialog` hide them in their own windows; `MainActivity` hides them again when it gets focus back). A swipe from the screen edge shows them for a moment.

The two nav corners (`Profile.corners`, shared by all workspaces) are always on the pad:

- tap ← / → for the previous / next workspace (dimmed to 35 % at an end, unless wrap-around is on);
- press both together for **fullscreen** (hides the top row); both again, or Back, to leave;
- their opacity (default 50 %) never goes below 25 % in play, since they are the way out of fullscreen;
- **Hide the ← / → corners after a few seconds** (default off): they fade 3 s after a reveal; four quick taps (≤300 ms apart) anywhere, or touching a corner, show them again;
- corners can be moved and sized in edit mode; "Same size for all corners" (default on) sizes both together.

## Edit mode

Opened by tapping the current workspace's capsule. It works on a **draft** of the profile, kept across activity recreation (up to 100 000 JSON characters). The pad shows the workspace being edited; controls are drawn at least 50 % opaque.

**Gestures**: tap a control to select it (taps on overlapping controls cycle through them); **long-press (450 ms)** opens its full-screen mapping editor; drag moves the selected control (with none selected, the one under the finger); two-finger pinch resizes (per axis when the fingers spread mostly one way); a two-finger tap selects nothing. At least 48 dp of a control must stay on screen. A tap on empty space selects the touchpad (opens its mapping editor on long press).

**The floating menu** (drag by its ⠿ dots, resize, fold):

- ↶ **Undo** / ↷ **Redo** (100 steps; changes within 400 ms merge into one step);
- The sliders icon (the same as the Defaults capsule's) saves the draft and opens the workspace's settings page;
- 🔒 **Lock layout**: drags and pinches stop moving or sizing what was there when edit mode opened (new controls stay free);
- **Done** saves (switches to the edited workspace); **Cancel** asks "Discard your changes?" if anything changed;
- **Select** (pick several controls, whole groups; a drag moves them all), **Group** / **Ungroup** (`ControlGroups`: grouped controls move and size together);
- **Configure** (the selected control's settings) and **Map** (its mapping editor);
- **Add +** opens the **Add** row under the menu's strip (**Add ▴** closes it), holding everything that can be added: **Button**, **Empty button** (every inherited input cleared), **Stick**, one entry per **gyro display** kind, then the **Library** (buttons saved to the button library, outlined; long-press one to remove it, asks first). Tap one to add it in the middle of the pad, or drag it down onto the pad to add it there; adding closes the row;
- **Snap: on/off** (edges on a 2 % grid);
- **Devices: <preset>**: which device preset this workspace pins ("None" leaves attached devices doing nothing);

New buttons start at 14 % of the short side, sticks 30 %.

## Mapping editor

`ui/ControlMappingScreen.kt`, full screen, for a touch control, the touchpad (the "Workspace" stand-in), the Defaults' mappings, or a physical device control. Its action **cards** (one per input, with their targets as chips) and a **library of targets** share the screen with a draggable divider. Tap a card, then a target to add it; or long-press and drag a target onto a card, a chip to another card, or back to the library to remove it. Cards reorder by their ⠿ handle. A chip opens its options (`ui/MappingChipDialog.kt`, `ui/ActionSettings.kt`): When, How, Where, Also ends on, delays, Cycle, scale, haptic feel. **Presets** fills in Windows touchpad or Gyro mouse. Layer chips on top pick Base or a layer; each shows only its own chips (no other set's, and in a layer nothing faded from the level above). In Base, inherited mappings show faded until the card maps anything itself; a faded chip dragged onto the library is removed (the card then overrides them); ✕, ↺, Reset all and Clear all work as in [Workspaces](phone-workspaces.md). **Settings** opens the control's settings (`ui/ControlSettingsScreen.kt`). **Duplicate** (copies its look and mappings), **Save to library** (for any workspace: add it from the edit panel's Library) and **Delete** (asks first) sit fixed at the top, outside the scrolling list: in the title row in landscape, under the title in portrait, as in workspace settings. Below them the categories: look (label, shape, size, layer, opacity), layers (follow layers), then the settings its actions share: touch surface, stick, gyro while held). Every change can be undone.

## What the pad shows

App-wide, App › Touch display (`ui/TouchVisuals.kt`), all off by default except where noted:

- **Show touches**: a dot under each finger that fades after it lifts;
- **Trail behind fingers**: a line of the finger's recent path, its end fading;
- **Show live mappings on buttons**: a button's label briefly shows what it sends (yellow, lingers 700 ms);
- **Swipe line**: a finger on a touchpad button shows a line from its start to its direction's activation lengths; only the arrows (↑ → ↓ ←) draw it; the Swipe card's Any line has none.
- **Move rulers**: a finger whose Move X or Y moves shows a sliding ruler per Move. 
- **Position ruler** (own switch in Touch display, default on): while a finger is on a button that maps its **Finger X**, **Y**, **distance** or **angle**, each shows a strip through the finger: X across the button, Y up it, distance from the centre out through the finger, angle as a ring round a round button (0 at the top, clockwise). A strip is clear in the input's start deadzone, a gradient from there, and full colour from its end deadzone; small ticks mark both edges. A label with a dark background shows what each of the button's finger mappings in use sends now ("Sens 4.70", "RX 62 %"), above the finger or, with **Values show: In the button's corner**, in its top-right corner. The colour is picked in the same settings (default orange). `PositionRulerOverlay` in `ui/TouchVisuals.kt`; values from `MappingEngine.fingerValueNow`.

A layer in use other than Base shows a pill for a second.

## Gyro displays

Touch controls of kind DISPLAY (`core/.../model/GyroDisplay.kt`, drawn by `ui/GyroVisuals.kt` in plain 2D, no 3D library), placed in edit mode, each with its own settings (`ui/GyroVisualsSettings.kt`):

- **3D phone** (the phone turned by the gyro);
- **Ratchet ring** (yaw/pitch/roll arcs, lit while that axis moves the mouse, dim while ratcheted);
- **Speed graph** (scrolling yaw/pitch/roll speed, ratchets shaded);
- **Attitude indicator** (aircraft horizon with heading tape);
- **Bubble disk** (heading disk with a tilt dot);
- **3D shape** (Globe, Icosphere, Cube, Tetrahedron, Dodecahedron, Torus, Gimbal rings, Paper plane, Camera);
- **Laser pointer** (`core/.../engine/LaserTrace.kt`, the paint): a see-through sphere seen from a fixed axonometric angle (30° round, 30° from above, no perspective, so a beam into the far side shows as a line, not a dot; **Axonometric view**, on by default, turned off shows it face-on instead; **Sphere turns, beam stays ahead**, off by default, keeps the beam pointing ahead (into the screen) and turns the sphere with its grid, axes and paint under it instead, the paint staying where it was painted on the sphere; **Sphere / Rifle / Crosshair** look: Rifle shows a shaded low-poly rifle aiming as the beam does instead of the sphere, grid, beam and dot, Draw mode still painting where it points, best seen in the axonometric view; Draw mode's **Fire** pick (a mouse button, controller button or LT/RT, or a key; default Mouse left; `LaserFire`) paints in the **Fire** to **Fire behind** colours while GyroPhone sends it (the Rifle look also shows a flickering **muzzle flash** in the Fire colour at its muzzle tip while it fires) (the PC overlay also while it's pressed on the PC's controller with "Use PC controller for gyro ratchet" on; the phone display can't see those presses), and **Paint only while firing** (off) paints only then; **Crosshair** shows only a crosshair lying on the sphere where the beam lands, facing its centre (so it squashes toward the edge), with the grid, axes and drawing as set: shape Cross, Cross with gap (default), Circle, Circle and cross, Dot or T (`LaserTrace.crosshair` / `CrosshairOn`), Size 2–30° (6°), Gap 0–10° (2°), Thickness 0.5–6 (2), Centre dot (on), and Outline (a dark edge, on); it turns about the beam only as the pose does (with Trackball, as the **Roll** row maps it, so None keeps it upright and Invert turns it the other way), coloured by the way the beam last moved (the **Moving** colours below; it keeps the last way's colour when the beam stops), dimmed while a ratchet is held), with its **15° grid** and its **axes** (the laser's own, turning with it: named as it aims, facing −Z into the screen: pitch X right red, yaw Y up green, roll Z along the beam blue, as in a normal map; both on by default) and, in the Sphere look only, **Gimbal rings** (off): yaw (green, outside), pitch (red) and roll (blue, innermost), one rigid set turning with the laser's axes, each round its own axis (`LaserTrace.gimbalRing` / `GimbalRing`), so each ring is the path the beam and crosshair take turning about it. With Trackball a None axis's ring is faint and dashed and its laser axis grey and dashed with a padlock at its tip (Camera's roll too), and an axis and its ring light up (thicker) while the gyro turns it (`GyroDisplayFeed.trackballActive` / `OverlayPose.Active`). So with the sphere still you see the laser's axes and rings turn, and with Sphere turns the sphere turns round the still beam and its rings. The laser at the centre aims as you aim the phone held as a gamepad, along its **roll axis** (out of its top edge; into the screen at rest, so a yaw turn swings it sideways and pitch up and down; with Turns with Camera, where the camera looks; with Trackball, **Laser faces** picks which of its directions it shines along, −Z (into the screen at rest, default), +Z, ±X or ±Y, so with the axis rows a trackball can match Phone or Camera): a beam to where it hits the inside, a dot there and a short **roll tick** pointing the way the phone's screen faces. The beam dims while a ratchet is held. **Draw mode** (off): the beam paints smooth glowing strokes on the sphere's inside as you aim (a thin bright line over a faint wide glow, **Brush** 1–20° wide, 4° by default; older strokes darker and fainter; each piece takes the colour of the way the beam moves there, blended as a normal map does (**way colours**, below); on the near half thinner and dimmer, seen through the shell), **Fades after** a slider from 0 (never, the default) to 60 s in 50 ms steps, finer toward the short end (the short ones work as a trail behind the beam; there is no separate trail); a held ratchet stops painting (no stroke is drawn across where the pose eased back). **Clear** wipes the strokes, and so does turning Draw mode off, or a ratchet press with **Clear on ratchet** on (off by default); a **Clear displays** mapping (Other targets) clears every laser pointer on the pad and the PC at once (the PC tray menu also has **Clear laser pointers**). Strokes are kept as points (`LaserTrace`): one each time the beam moved 0.5° or more, up to 4096 (the oldest go first), drawn as a few paths, one per strength, so they cost the same however long you paint. Strokes are not saved. **Way colours** (paint and crosshair, `LaserTrace.directionColor`): the Colours list has **Moving right / left / up / down / toward you / away**, the colour of each straight way the beam moves in the sphere's frame (x right, y up, z toward you, as the sphere sits when not turned), by default a normal map's (pink, teal, light green, purple, blue, olive: 0.5 + 0.5 × the way per red, green, blue); a slanted move blends them, each way's share taking the colour from the middle (the six colours' average) toward its own, so a straight move shows exactly its colour. **Smooth way colours** (on) takes each stroke's way over ±2° of its line (`LaserTrace.Ring.direction`) and eases the crosshair's over 60 ms, so small shakes don't flicker; off, each 0.5° step is coloured by itself.

Their settings are laid out as the [PC overlays'](pc-overlays.md#per-window-settings) are, with the same names, ranges and defaults (a new display starts as a new PC overlay does):

- **Look**: the kind's own options (3D phone: ghost trail, axis arrows, axonometric view; Bubble disk: dot on the rim at 5–90°, crosshair; 3D shape: shape, subdivision, smooth, hidden edges, filled faces, equator, screen and top marks, axonometric view (seen from the laser pointer's fixed angle with the world's axes, instead of face-on); speed graph: range and seconds; laser pointer: axonometric view, grid, axes, Draw mode, brush, fade, Clear), opacity, then **Colours** (tap a colour to pick it; Reset / Default colours).
- **Turning** (the kinds that show the pose): **Turns with**: **Phone** (default: how the phone is held), **Camera** (where the game's camera points, turning as a game camera does: left and right about the vertical and up and down about its own right, pitch within ±89°, never rolling; the gyro axis that moves the mouse sideways turns it left and right and the one on mouse Y up and down (`GyroDisplayFeed.cameraMapOf`, sent to the PC in OVERLAY bytes 149 and 150), with every other camera move, touchpad, buttons, controllers, keys, glides; a ratchet stops it as it stops the camera) or **Trackball** (turned by the gyro and every other camera move: for each of its axes, Yaw (Y, up: turning left and right), Pitch (X, right) and Roll (Z, along the laser's beam, through the screen at rest), you pick which gyro turn turns it (Yaw / Pitch / Roll / None) and can invert it; **None** holds that axis at 0 whatever the others do, as Camera never rolls (saved Lock flags from older versions read as None). **Turns about**: **The sphere's axes** (default) turns it as a gimbal, left and right about the sphere's vertical, then up and down about its own right, then about where it faces (`GyroPose.gimbalTurn`, `OverlayPose.GimbalTurn`), and each row has **Stop at ±90°**: Pitch (on by default) stops at straight up or down (±89°, the poles), and without it goes over the top with left and right staying as seen; Yaw and Roll (off by default) stop once turned sideways. **Its own axes** turns it about its own axes as it now points (left and right about its own up, up and down about its own right, `GyroPose.turnOwn`, `OverlayPose.Turn`), so the crosshair moves at right angles as it sees it and never rolls by itself; there are no poles, so Stop at ±90° is hidden. Moving in a loop leaves it a little rolled (the sphere's curvature: a loop's twist equals its area), so picking it also turns on Roll's ease back When the phone stops, which levels that out. By default it turns as Camera does with the default gyro mouse: the gyro's yaw turns it left and right, pitch up and down (stopping at the poles), Roll None, about the sphere's axes, and **As the camera** puts that back); Camera and Trackball turn as far as the game's camera (when its turn is known) or Spin 0.1–10× times the phone, have no Gravity, Compass or Turn amount, and Level sets them straight; a Trackball has no overall Ease back: instead each axis row has **Ease <axis> axis back** with its own options (On ratchet or When the phone stops, Delay, By time or By speed; default off), easing just that axis to straight (`GyroDisplay.trackballEase`, `OverlaySettings.TrackballEase`). **Pose** (Phone; with Camera only Ease back): **Gravity** (default on), **Compass** (with gravity), **Ease back to straight** (on): **On ratchet** (default: while a ratchet is held it eases back until straight, stopping when let go) or **When the phone stops**, Heading only (without gravity), Delay (300 ms), By time (2 s) or By speed (90 °/s); with the compass the heading doesn't ease back and a ratchet press aims it again. **Amount**: **Turn amount** (0.5–3×; not on a trackball, which turns by Spin) and **Level**: hold the phone the way you play and press it; with Gravity that tilt (read from the accelerometer then) shows face-on, saved as the display's resting grip; without, the pose starts again from straight. A **Level displays** mapping (Other targets, on any button) does the same for every display on the pad and every PC overlay at once, in play. The heading is measured from that grip, by where the screen faces (the edges take over near flat), as on the PC (`GyroPose.headingFrom`).

A saved trackball 3D shape becomes Turns with Trackball (with Invert on, from test builds, Camera). Displays saved before this layout carry over as the PC's did (`GyroDisplay.migrated`, on load): Reset on ratchet becomes Ease on ratchet (with its Heading only), "When the ratchet is let go" becomes When the phone stops, and the Level angle becomes the matching resting grip.

Displays that hold their pose to gravity run the accelerometer; ones that hold heading to north run the magnetometer. The **parallax edge** (App › Gyro display) draws outlines inside the screen edge that slide as the phone turns (Turn) or shift while tilted (Tilt).

## Debug panel

While the PC's latency test window is open, the pad shows the test's tracking markers over everything (touches still go through; **Hide markers** hides them until the next test), see [Latency test](pc-latency-test.md#latency-test-page).

With App › Debugging › **Debug tools** on (default off: nothing is logged), `ui/DebugOverlay.kt` floats over the pad: link, gyro, workspace and layer, what is held (inputs and outputs) and the newest events. Drag the title to move it, ◢ to resize, ▾ to fold it. **Record** saves the last 30 s (`runtime/PhoneDebug.kt`).
