# Phone: pad screen and edit mode

What the person sees and touches: the pad, its top row, the ← / → corners, edit mode and the mapping editor. What touches do is in [Mapping system](phone-mapping.md); settings pages are in [Settings](phone-settings.md).

## The app is the pad

`ui/MainActivity.kt` shows `ui/PadMenu.kt` `PadTab`: the pad (`ui/PadScreen.kt`) fills the screen, and every other page (Controller, Settings, Connection, a workspace's settings) opens in its place. Back steps out one page at a time. The pad stays composed under an open page, neither drawn nor touchable (`PadTab`'s `KeptUnder`), so coming back shows it at once with its caches; while hidden it counts as off screen (`setPadShown(false)`: everything held is released, and with **Send input only while the pad is open** the gyro pauses), holds the profile it showed until it is back, and stops its polls, frame loops, gyro displays, screen-on and mouse capture. There are no other tabs.

**Play mode** draws the active workspace's controls in its placement for the current orientation (landscape and portrait placements are kept apart; a control with no portrait placement gets one derived from landscape, squeezed into the lower part with the top left to the touchpad). Empty space is the workspace touchpad. A label too long for its control shrinks to fit (down to 7 sp), never cut off with an ellipsis. Each finger on a stick owns it until it lifts; a finger on a button goes to the engine; overlapping controls are allowed (front one wins unless a control behind has "shares touches"; order by size, or Front/Back). Play mode asks Android for unbuffered touch and joystick input (`TouchStream`) and passes the event time on, so the engine sees touches at their real time. A system cancel (an OxygenOS three-finger gesture taking the pointers) is handled phone-side: every finger is let go cleanly.

## Top row (capsules)

`ui/CapsuleRow.kt`, shown in play mode when not in fullscreen:

- **+** adds a workspace at the front (Blank, Windows touchpad or a preset, see [Workspaces](phone-workspaces.md)).
- One capsule per **workspace**: tap to switch; tap the current one to open its edit mode.
- **Controller** (the device screens, [Controllers](phone-controllers.md)), **App** (gear: the whole app's settings), **Defaults** (sliders: what every workspace follows, [Settings](phone-settings.md)), **Connection** (shows the link state).

The row scrolls. Long-press a capsule and drag it to reorder; moving workspaces reorders them. The order is stored as a string of `W`/`D`/`S` (App)/`F` (Defaults)/`C`; an order saved before Defaults existed gets it right after App.

## ← / → corners and fullscreen

The status and navigation bars never show in the app, on the pad, in settings or in any dialog (`ui/AppDialogs.kt`: the app's `Dialog` and `AlertDialog` hide them in their own windows; `MainActivity` hides them again when it gets focus back). A swipe from the screen edge shows them for a moment.

The two nav corners (`Profile.corners`, shared by all workspaces) are always on the pad:

- tap ← / → for the previous / next workspace (dimmed to 35 % at an end, unless wrap-around is on);
- press both together for **fullscreen** (hides the top row); both again, or Back, to leave;
- their opacity (default 50 %) never goes below 25 % in play, since they are the way out of fullscreen;
- **Hide the ← / → corners after a few seconds** (default off): they fade 3 s after a reveal; four quick taps (≤300 ms apart) anywhere, or touching a corner, show them again;
- corners can be moved and sized in edit mode; "Same size for all corners" (default on) sizes both together.

## Edit mode

Opened by tapping the current workspace's capsule. It works on a **draft** of the profile, kept across activity recreation (up to 100 000 JSON characters). The pad shows the workspace being edited; controls are drawn at least 50 % opaque.

**Gestures**: tap a control to select it; **tap the selected control again** to open its full-screen mapping editor (a gyro display: its settings; a ← / → corner: its menu). On overlapping controls a tap acts on the selected one when it is under the finger, else picks the front one. **Long-press (450 ms)** a control to select it and show its **popup** beside it: **Mappings** (not on a gyro display), **Duplicate**, **Save as preset**, **Ungroup** (a grouped control only) and **Delete** (red; asks "Delete "<name>"?" first, removes only that control, Undo brings it back); a corner's long press opens its menu. A tap on empty space closes an open popup, else opens the touchpad's mapping editor; a long press there does nothing. Drag moves the selected control (with none selected, the one under the finger; a drag also closes the popup); two-finger pinch resizes (per axis when the fingers spread mostly one way); a two-finger tap selects nothing. At least 48 dp of a control must stay on screen.

A control's **mapping editor** and its **settings** (⚙ Settings in the editor, Mappings or Configure in the settings) open over each other: the one opened second lies on top, and its Mappings / Settings button or Done goes back to the one under it, so the pad never shows between them (each is its own window).

**The floating menu** (drag by its ⠿ dots; drag its **bottom-right corner grip** sideways to resize it, down to wrap its strip into up to 4 rows, which then scrolls up and down instead of sideways; the Add / Presets row stays one sideways row so its items still drag down onto the pad):

- ↶ **Undo** / ↷ **Redo** (100 steps; changes within 400 ms merge into one step);
- The sliders icon (the same as the Defaults capsule's) saves the draft and opens the workspace's settings page;
- 🔒 **Lock layout**: drags and pinches stop moving or sizing what was there when edit mode opened (new controls stay free);
- **Done** saves (switches to the edited workspace); **Cancel** asks "Discard your changes?" if anything changed;
- **Select** (pick several controls, whole groups; a drag moves them all), **Group** / **Ungroup** (`ControlGroups`: grouped controls move and size together);
- **Configure** (the selected control's settings) and **Map** (its mapping editor);
- **Add +** opens the **Add** row under the menu's strip (**Add ▴** closes it), holding everything that can be added: **Button**, **Empty button** (every inherited input cleared), **Stick**, and one entry per **gyro display** kind. **Add preset ★** beside it opens the **Presets** row instead: your saved buttons, sticks, gyro displays and groups (the button library, each with a ★, outlined; empty, a hint says where to save one); long-press one to **Rename** or **Delete** it. Tap one to add it in the middle of the pad, or drag it down onto the pad to add it there; adding closes the row. A saved group is added as a new group keeping its shape, its first member where you drop it;
- **Save as preset** shows in the strip when a group is selected (a grouped control, or one whole group picked in Select mode): it asks a name ("Group of N") and saves every member with its look, settings and mappings (`ButtonLibrary.saveGroup`, `LibraryButton.more`). A saved group is only for Add preset, never a mapping editor preset;
- **Snap: on/off** (edges on a 2 % grid);
- **Devices: <preset>**: which device preset this workspace pins ("None" leaves attached devices doing nothing);

New buttons start at 14 % of the short side, sticks 30 %.

## Mapping editor

`ui/ControlMappingScreen.kt`, full screen, for a touch control, the touchpad (the "Workspace" stand-in), the Defaults' mappings, or a physical device control. Its action **cards** (one per input, with their targets as chips) and a **library of targets** share the screen with a draggable divider. Tap a card, then a target to add it; or long-press and drag a target onto a card, a chip to another card, or back to the library to remove it. Cards reorder by their ⠿ handle. A chip opens its options (`ui/MappingChipDialog.kt`, `ui/ActionSettings.kt`): When, How, Where, Also ends on, delays, Cycle, scale, haptic feel. **Presets** (the library's Presets tab; see below). Layer chips on top pick Base or a layer; each shows only its own chips (no other set's, and in a layer nothing faded from the level above). In Base, inherited chips show faded beside the card's own; tapping one or dragging it onto the library (hidden actions) asks Override (an Off for that chip) or Replace (an own copy); ✕, ↺, Reset all and Clear all work as in [Workspaces](phone-workspaces.md). In portrait the top bar has two rows: the title (with Done) on the first and the layer chips, then Sort, Reset all, Clear all, + Preset, Settings and Undo on the second. In landscape it is one thin row scrolling sideways: the label first (without the kind line under it), then the layer chips and the rest, with Done kept at its right end. **The title is the label field**: tap the control's name at the top to type its label (keyboard emoji work), and **☺** next to it adds symbols (arrows, keys, pad buttons, game icons) or clears the label; empty shows the mapping. For the touchpad the title edits the touchpad's label, and a **Show on pad** chip beside it shows that label at the top centre of the pad in play, where, like a button's label, it briefly shows what the touchpad sends (yellow) when live mappings are on (off by default; `Workspace.touchpadLabel`, `touchpadLabelShown`). **Settings** opens the control's settings (`ui/ControlSettingsScreen.kt`). **Duplicate** (copies its look and mappings), **Save as preset** (for any workspace: add it from the edit panel's Add preset) and **Delete** (asks first) sit fixed at the top, outside the scrolling list: in the title row in landscape, under the title in portrait, as in workspace settings. Below them the categories: look (shape, size, layer, opacity), layers (follow layers), then the settings its actions share: touch surface, stick, gyro while held). Every change can be undone.


### Presets in the mapping editor

Presets come at three levels, each with its own icon (a legend sits at the top of the Presets tab): a **chip** preset (a filled pill: one target with its chip options), a **card** preset (a card with a pill: everything one input card maps, plus that card's settings) and a **button** preset (a circle around a card: a whole control's mappings, plus its settings). Built-in ones: chip presets for common keys and mouse buttons (Mouse left/right, Space, Shift, Ctrl, Esc, Tab, Enter, Ratchet; only on controls with a press), stick presets, Windows touchpad and Mouse cursor (touch controls with swipes) and Gyro mouse. Button presets are the **button library**: a button saved with **+ Preset** (or Save to library) is both.

- **Saving one**: a chip's options have **Save as chip preset**; a card's **+** (with the card icon, shown when the card maps something of its own) saves it as a card preset; **+ Preset** in the top bar saves the whole control as a button preset (a touch control goes to the library, which also lists it in edit mode's Add preset row; a physical button or the touchpad is a button preset only, mappings without settings). The editor asks for a name. Presets are app-wide (`files/mapping_presets.json`, `core/.../model/MappingPresets.kt`).
- **Where they fit**: a chip preset fits any card that takes its target; a card preset only cards of the same kind it was made from (a tap card on any button's tap card, a stick's ↑ on any stick's ↑, a physical key on any key); a button preset only controls of the same kind. Presets that don't fit the selected card are greyed, and while one is dragged the cards it doesn't fit are greyed.
- **Dragging** a chip preset onto a card adds it there, never replacing anything. Dragging a card preset onto a card, or a button preset onto the cards, opens the popup below.
- **Tapping** any preset opens the popup instead of applying at once: **Add** (beside what is there; an identical chip is not added twice) or **Replace** (asks to confirm; clears that card, or the whole control for a button preset, first). The popup's **Apply with settings** switch (on by default) also brings the card's or button's settings (Tap time, deadzone, zones, surface, gyro while held...); off brings only the chips with their own options. **Rename** and **Delete** of your own presets are in the same popup.
- **No false overrides**: a setting is written only where it changes what the control does (`MappingPresets.applySettings`), and a chip equal to what the card already inherits is not written as its own, so nothing new shows as overriding the Defaults or the workspace. Every apply can be undone.

## What the pad shows

App-wide, App › Touch display (`ui/TouchVisuals.kt`), all off by default except where noted:

- **Show touches**: a dot under each finger that fades after it lifts;
- **Trail behind fingers**: a line of the finger's recent path, its end fading;
- **Show live mappings on buttons**: a button's label briefly shows what it sends (yellow, lingers 700 ms);
- **Swipe line**: a finger on a touchpad button shows a line from its start to its direction's activation lengths; only the arrows (↑ → ↓ ←) draw it; the Swipe card's Any line has none.
- **Move rulers**: a finger whose Move X or Y moves shows a sliding ruler per Move. 
- **Position ruler** (own switch in Touch display, default on): while a finger is on a button that maps its **Finger X**, **Y**, **distance** or **angle**, each shows a strip through the finger: X across the button, Y up it, distance from the centre out through the finger, angle as a ring round a round button (0 at the top, clockwise). A strip is clear in the input's start deadzone, a gradient from there, and full colour from its end deadzone; small ticks mark both edges. A label with a dark background shows what each of the button's finger mappings in use sends now ("Sens 4.70", "RX 62 %"), above the finger or, with **Values show: In the button's corner**, in its top-right corner. The colour is picked in the same settings (default orange). It follows the finger frame by frame only while a finger is on a button, reading every finger and value under one engine lock, and draws again only when one moved; each label is laid out again only when its text changed. `PositionRulerOverlay` in `ui/TouchVisuals.kt`; values from `MappingEngine.fingerRuler`.

A layer in use other than Base shows a pill for a second.

## Gyro displays

Touch controls of kind DISPLAY (`core/.../model/GyroDisplay.kt`, drawn by `ui/GyroVisuals.kt` in plain 2D, no 3D library), placed in edit mode, each with its own settings (`ui/GyroVisualsSettings.kt`). Its **label** is edited in place in the settings' title (as a button's in the mapping editor, with the ☺ symbol picker); empty, the title shows the kind's name greyed. The label only names it there, in the delete question and as a preset: the display never draws it, and the PC's phone overlay gets no label for it. **Save as preset** (beside Duplicate) asks a name (the label, else the kind's name) and saves the display's look, settings and size to Add preset, not its place.

- **3D phone** (the phone turned by the gyro);
- **Ratchet ring** (yaw/pitch/roll arcs, lit while that axis moves the mouse, dim while ratcheted);
- **Speed graph** (scrolling yaw/pitch/roll speed, ratchets shaded);
- **Attitude indicator** (aircraft horizon with heading tape);
- **Bubble disk** (heading disk with a tilt dot);
- **3D shape** (Globe, Icosphere, Cube, Tetrahedron, Dodecahedron, Torus, Gimbal rings, Paper plane, Camera);

With Trackball a None axis's ring is faint and dashed and its laser axis grey and dashed with a padlock at its tip (Camera's roll too), and an axis and its ring light up (thicker) while the gyro turns it (`GyroDisplayFeed.trackballActive` / `OverlayPose.Active`). So with the sphere still you see the laser's axes and rings turn, and in Fixed pointer mode the sphere turns round the still beam and its rings. The laser at the centre aims as you aim the phone held as a gamepad, along its **roll axis** (out of its top edge; into the screen at rest, so a yaw turn swings it sideways and pitch up and down; with Turns with Camera, where the camera looks; with Trackball, **Pointer faces** picks which of its directions it shines along, −Z (into the screen at rest, default), +Z, ±X or ±Y, so with the axis rows a trackball can match Phone or Camera): a beam to where it hits the inside, a dot there and a short **roll tick** pointing the way the phone's screen faces. The beam dims while a ratchet is held. **Draw mode** (off): the beam paints the sphere's inside as you aim (**Brush** 1–20°, 4° by default, the paint 0.6° wide per Brush degree). The paint is a map of the whole sphere (`LaserPaint`, by default 1024 × 512 texels by longitude and latitude in the sphere's frame): each new stroke point (one each time the beam moved 0.5° or more) stamps the line from the point before, each texel keeping its amount of paint, how much of that is shots (Fire) and a wide faint glow amount (out to three half widths), all three bytes handed to the GPU as alpha-only bitmaps and blended smoothly between texels (and, with Colour by the way it moved, the way colour times the amount). Paint builds up like real paint: each line lays its soft-edged paint over what is there (a + b·(1 − a)), its colours mixing in by their share, so crossing lines and their colours blend; within one line the overlapping stamps take the line's strongest at each texel, not their sum, so the line stays even. **Softness** shapes the paint as it is laid down (only new strokes change). A GPU shader (`PointerPaintShader`, AGSL) draws it: every pixel of the sphere's picture finds the point of the sphere it shows (in perspective with Perspective on, else flat; near half and far half drawn separately in the usual order) and reads the map round it, so the paint is smooth, foreshortened toward the rim and bigger nearer exactly as paint on a ball, at the same cost however much is painted. **Paint** tab (shown with Draw mode on): **Colour by** **Where it is** (default: the paint takes the colour of where it is in the scene, the Moving colours laid as a normal map fixed to the scene, not to the sphere, so in Fixed pointer mode they stay put like light while the sphere turns) or **The way it moved** (each texel keeps the colour of the way the beam moved when it painted it, as before); shots take the **Fire** to **Fire behind** colours by where they land, aiming the **Aim** to **Aim behind** colours; **Softness** 0–100% (50%) how soft the paint's edge is (for new strokes); **Glow** 0–100% (50%) a faint glow round it (0 none); **Outline** (off) a thin dark edge round it in the **Paint outline** colour; **Facing shade** 0–100% (50%) darkens paint where the surface turns away from you (at full, to a quarter at the rim), so the paint shows the sphere's roundness; **Inside** 20–100% (70%) the far half's paint, seen from inside the sphere, at that share of the near half's brightness (seen from outside through the shell); **Light**: a light fixed in the scene (it stays put while the sphere or pointer turns) from **Upper left** (default), Above, Upper right or Front, lighting the side you see (outside on the near half, inside on the far), **Light strength** 0–100% (50%) darkening what faces away from it (0 off), **Shine** 0–100% (30%) a glossy highlight where it reflects toward you; the Snake is lit the same way; **Line smoothing** below. **Snake** (off, a switch of its own beside Draw mode, working with or without it): a line along the newest **Length** of the beam's path (10–720°, 180° by default, in 5° steps, finer toward the short end; counted along the lines, the jumps between them adding nothing; `LaserTrace.Ring.cut` keeps only that much of the stroke points), **Snake brush** (1–20°, 4°, its own, apart from Draw mode's Brush, which shows only with Draw mode on; shots and aiming keep the Fire and Aim brushes) wide at its head, thinning by **Taper** (0–100%, 80%: at 80% the tail is a fifth of the head; 0 keeps one width) and fading by **Tail fade** (0–100%, 50%: how see-through its tail is; 0 none) toward its tail, with a soft **Snake glow** (0–100%, off) round it (three wider, fainter copies under it), bigger nearer with Perspective, coloured and shaded as the paint (Colour by, Fire colours, Facing shade, Inside). It runs along a smooth curve through the path's points (`LaserTrace.curve`, a centripetal Catmull-Rom spline on the sphere, which never loops or overshoots), cut into steps of at most 0.5° with its colour and width blending along each, so a fast turn, whose points land several degrees apart, still draws round, with no corners or visible joints; its pieces go newest first into one layer, each only where the layer is still empty, so where the snake crosses itself the newer part shows and nothing darkens or shows a joint; the layer is laid on at the colours' opacity. Draw mode's paint stays until cleared; the map holds one byte per texel (handed to the GPU as an alpha-only bitmap, uploaded only when it changes, and while the beam paints at most about 30 times a second (`PointerPaintShader.UPLOAD_MS`); the beam, dot and Snake still move every frame, and a new or cleared map goes up at once; the way colours are converted only for the rows painted since the last upload, `LaserPaint.changedFrom` .. `changedTo`). **Paint detail** (Paint tab, Draw mode only) Low (512 × 256 texels, lightest on the battery), **Normal** (1024 × 512, default) or High (2048 × 1024, sharpest, best on the charger); changing it clears the paint. A held ratchet stops painting (no stroke is drawn across where the pose eased back). **Clear** wipes the paint, and so does turning Draw mode off, or a ratchet press with **Clear on ratchet** on (off by default); a **Clear displays** mapping (Other targets) clears every pointer on the pad and the PC at once (the PC tray menu also has **Clear pointers**). The paint is not saved. **Way colours** (the crosshair, and the paint with Colour by The way it moved, `LaserTrace.directionColor`): the Colours list has **Moving right / left / up / down / toward you / away**, the colour of each straight way the beam moves in the sphere's frame (x right, y up, z toward you, as the sphere sits when not turned), by default a normal map's (pink, teal, light green, purple, blue, olive: 0.5 + 0.5 × the way per red, green, blue); a slanted move blends them, each way's share taking the colour from the middle (the six colours' average) toward its own, so a straight move shows exactly its colour. **Smooth way colours** (on) takes each stroke's way over the 2° of line before it (`LaserTrace.Ring.direction`) and eases the crosshair's over 60 ms, so small shakes don't flicker; off, each 0.5° step is coloured by itself. **Line smoothing** off–100% (off): the paint follows a steadied beam (`LaserTrace.steadyToward`), like a drawing app's stabiliser: up to 150 ms behind at full strength when the beam moves slowly, easing off with its speed (evened out over 50 ms so a shake back and forth cancels; twice as fast at 60°/s), each new line starting on the beam; the beam, dot, crosshair and way colours of the crosshair stay raw.

Their settings are laid out as the [PC overlays'](pc-overlays.md#per-window-settings) are, with the same names, ranges and defaults (a new display starts as a new PC overlay does):

- The pointer, having much to set, splits its settings into more tabs: **Look** (look, view, grid, axes, gimbal rings, Fire display and Aim display (a row each showing the picked button; tapping it opens a popup in two tabs, Keyboard & mouse and Gamepad, their buttons as chips wrapped to the screen width, scrolling if they don't fit, with None on top) with their brushes, glows, kick, muzzle flash and crosshair size, Paint only while firing or aiming, opacity), **Draw** (Draw mode, Snake with its length, Snake brush, Taper, Tail fade and Snake glow, Draw mode's brush, clear on ratchet, Clear), **Paint** (only with Draw mode or Snake: colour by, paint detail, softness, glow, outline (these three Draw mode only), facing shade, inside, line smoothing), **Colours** and **Turning**. The other kinds keep Look (with their colours) and Turning.
- **Look**: the kind's own options (3D phone: ghost trail, axis arrows, axonometric view, perspective (on: nearer parts bigger, as a camera sees them; off: flat; also on the 3D shape and the pointer); Bubble disk: dot on the rim at 5–90°, crosshair; 3D shape: shape, subdivision, smooth, hidden edges, filled faces, equator, screen and top marks, axonometric view (seen from the pointer's fixed angle with the world's axes, instead of face-on), perspective; speed graph: range and seconds; pointer: axonometric view, perspective, grid, axes, Draw mode, brush, fade, Clear), opacity, then **Colours** (tap a colour to pick it; Reset / Default colours).
- **Turning** (the kinds that show the pose): **Turns with**: **Phone** (default: how the phone is held), **Camera** (where the game's camera points, turning as a game camera does: left and right about the vertical and up and down about its own right, pitch within ±89°, never rolling; the gyro axis that moves the mouse sideways turns it left and right and the one on mouse Y up and down (`GyroDisplayFeed.cameraMapOf`, sent to the PC in OVERLAY bytes 149 and 150), with every other camera move, touchpad, buttons, controllers, keys, glides; a ratchet stops it as it stops the camera) or **Trackball** (turned by the gyro and every other camera move: for each of its axes, Yaw (Y, up: turning left and right), Pitch (X, right) and Roll (Z, along the laser's beam, through the screen at rest), you pick which gyro turn turns it (Yaw / Pitch / Roll / None) and can invert it; **None** holds that axis at 0 whatever the others do, as Camera never rolls (saved Lock flags from older versions read as None). **Turns about**: **The sphere's axes** (default) turns it as a gimbal, left and right about the sphere's vertical, then up and down about its own right, then about where it faces (`GyroPose.gimbalTurn`, `OverlayPose.GimbalTurn`), and each row has **Stop at ±90°**: Pitch (on by default) stops at straight up or down (±89°, the poles), and without it goes over the top with left and right staying as seen; Yaw and Roll (off by default) stop once turned sideways. **Its own axes** turns it about its own axes as it now points (left and right about its own up, up and down about its own right, `GyroPose.turnOwn`, `OverlayPose.Turn`), so the crosshair moves at right angles as it sees it and never rolls by itself; there are no poles, so Stop at ±90° is hidden. Moving in a loop leaves it a little rolled (the sphere's curvature: a loop's twist equals its area), so picking it also turns on Roll's ease back When the phone stops, which levels that out. By default it turns as Camera does with the default gyro mouse: the gyro's yaw turns it left and right, pitch up and down (stopping at the poles), Roll None, about the sphere's axes, and **As the camera** puts that back); Camera and Trackball turn as far as the game's camera (when its turn is known) or Spin 0.1–10× times the phone, have no Gravity, Compass or Turn amount, and Level sets them straight; a Trackball has no overall Ease back: instead each axis row has **Ease <axis> axis back** with its own options (On ratchet or When the phone stops, Delay, By time or By speed; default off), easing just that axis to straight (`GyroDisplay.trackballEase`, `OverlaySettings.TrackballEase`). **Pose** (Phone; with Camera only Ease back): **Gravity** (default on), **Compass** (with gravity), **Ease back to straight** (on): **On ratchet** (default: while a ratchet is held it eases back until straight, stopping when let go) or **When the phone stops**, Heading only (without gravity), Delay (300 ms), By time (2 s) or By speed (90 °/s); with the compass the heading doesn't ease back and a ratchet press aims it again. **Amount**: **Turn amount** (0.5–3×; not on a trackball, which turns by Spin) and **Level**: hold the phone the way you play and press it; with Gravity that tilt (read from the accelerometer then) shows face-on, saved as the display's resting grip; without, the pose starts again from straight. A **Set orientation** mapping (Other targets, on any button) sets the workspace's base orientation (Yaw axis) from how the phone is held and starts every display on the pad again from it, level, and levels every PC overlay, in play. In World and Player gyro space the displays show the phone's real pose against gravity, about the screen's own axes, with no saved grip. The heading is measured from that grip, by where the screen faces (the edges take over near flat), as on the PC (`GyroPose.headingFrom`).

A saved trackball 3D shape becomes Turns with Trackball (with Invert on, from test builds, Camera). Displays saved before this layout carry over as the PC's did (`GyroDisplay.migrated`, on load): Reset on ratchet becomes Ease on ratchet (with its Heading only), "When the ratchet is let go" becomes When the phone stops, and the Level angle becomes the matching resting grip.

Displays that hold their pose to gravity run the accelerometer; ones that hold heading to north run the magnetometer. The **parallax edge** (App › Gyro display) draws outlines inside the screen edge that slide as the phone turns (Turn) or shift while tilted (Tilt).

Drawing: the displays are drawn again only when what they show changed: a pose display once its shown pose moved by about 0.05° (so sensor noise on a still phone draws nothing), a graph's or pointer's data, the live gyro axes, a held ratchet, a trackball's lit axes, fades and aim easing, and for a moment after (so ghost trails catch up). The parallax edge is drawn again once it moved a quarter pixel (or turned a hundredth of a degree, or its tint changed). The displays, the edge and each touch overlay (touches and trails, swipe lines, move rulers, position ruler) are drawn on layers of their own, so one redrawing leaves the others alone. Under the black screen or the latency markers none of them draws ([Volume rocker and black screen](phone-volume-black-screen.md)).

## Debug panel

While the PC's latency test window is open, the pad shows the test's tracking markers over everything (touches still go through; **Hide markers** hides them until the next test), see [Latency test](pc-latency-test.md#latency-test-page).

With App › Debugging › **Debug tools** on (default off: nothing is logged), `ui/DebugOverlay.kt` floats over the pad: link, gyro, workspace and layer, what is held (inputs and outputs) and the newest events. Drag the title to move it, ◢ to resize, ▾ to fold it. **Record** saves the last 30 s (`runtime/PhoneDebug.kt`) to the app's external files, `Android/data/com.oleroma.gyrophone/files/debug/`, where `tools/debug-collect.sh` pulls it over adb (no run-as: the phone's build isn't debuggable). Its live lines update every 200 ms (not under the black screen or the latency markers); the event list is one text, made again only when a new event came or what it shows changed (kinds, timings, pause).
