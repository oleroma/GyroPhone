# PC: overlays

Windows on the PC that show the phone's motion over a game or in a stream.

## Windows and kinds

Any number of overlay windows (`OverlaySettings`, Overlays page › Add), each one of:

- **Phone**: the phone in 3D with the workspace's layout, presses and fingers on its screen, shown as the pad shows them: a control lights while one of its mappings is active (a button with only a long press lights when it fires), and each button's frame (its mappings active now, its Toggles on or off, its sliders' values, in its middle in place of its label or outside it by its top-right corner), sliders icon and Position bars show as on the pad, following the pad's own switches (Show mappings on buttons, Show sliders on buttons, Slider colour). The phone sends them in their own packet (OVERLAY_FRAMES, [protocol](protocol.md)) only while the PC's overlay is on, when they change and every second (`MarkDrawing`); optional Switch-style **controller handles** on its sides (narrow, sticks and buttons centred, −/+/Home next to the phone edge, bumper/trigger pills on the front, presses shown visually, no labels; every button, D-pad arrow, −/+/Home and stick knob is a raised 3D shape that sinks almost flush and lights while it fires, the bumpers sink into the top edge and the triggers into the back as far as they are pulled). Handles: **While a controller is attached** (default) / Always / Off; **Controller**: Phone (default) or PC (the PC's controller, only while the phone's PC ratchet switch is on).
- **3D shape**: Globe (default), Icosphere (subdivision 0–3), Cube, Tetrahedron, Dodecahedron, Torus, Gimbal rings, Paper plane, Camera (seen from behind, lens pointing where it looks); smooth edges, hidden edges, filled faces, equator, screen and top marks; **Axonometric view** (off).
- **Axonometric view** (Phone and 3D shape, off by default): seen from the laser pointer's fixed angle (30° round, 30° from above) instead of face-on, with the world's axes (yaw toward you red, pitch right green, roll up blue, as the gimbal's rings) through it; the drawing shrinks a little so the axes fit.
- **Attitude indicator**: horizon with a heading tape.
- **Bubble disk**: heading disk with a tilt dot (range 30°), crosshair.
- **Ratchet ring** (`GaugeDrawing`, as the phone's): yaw (top), pitch and roll arcs with their letters, lit while that axis moves the mouse and dim while a ratchet (or the gyro mode) pauses it. Colours Yaw, Pitch, Roll, Letters. New ones start 120 px square.
- **Speed graph** (`GaugeDrawing`, `GyroPhone.Host/SpeedTrace.cs`, as the phone's): scrolling yaw, pitch and roll speed, newest at the right, ±**Range** full height (±180/360/720/1440 °/s, 360) over the last **Seconds** (2/3/5/8 s, 3), shaded while a ratchet is held. The speeds come from every gyro sample (OVERLAY_SAMPLES, asked for while a Speed graph is shown; the frame's own turn when they are missing), averaged into 1/120 s buckets. Colours Yaw, Pitch, Roll, Background, Ratchet shading. New ones start 420×115 px. Neither it nor the ring turns with the phone, so they have no Turning tab.
- **FPS** (`FpsDrawing`): the game's frames per second big, then the last minute's 1% low, jumps and worst frame, the game's name, and the last 10 s of frame times as a strip (the longest frame per pixel column, a line at 60 fps, jumps as dots); the edge lights in the Jumps colour for a second after a jump. The game's name and, while Diagnostics is on (Performance), "● DIAG" and how long it has been on in the Jumps colour ("● DIAG 4:12") sit in a row of their own on top. While Diagnostics is on, a graph of the link's last 60 s goes under the frame times: a bar per second as high as its latest packet's delay (0–100 ms, a stub for a clean second), full height in the Jumps colour for a second with lost packets, phone pauses or let-go keys, with the newest second's latency, slowest 1% and late count written over it. Colours: Numbers, Frame times, Jumps, Panel (a dark see-through panel). It is the only thing that watches the game's frames: while one is added and switched on ([Performance](pc-companion.md#performance)); it needs nothing from the phone (an FPS overlay alone doesn't ask the phone for overlay data, and phone frames never make it draw), draws 4 times a second, never fades when the phone rests, and has no Turning tab.
- **Keys** (`KeysDrawing`): the keys of the controller attached to the phone that fire now and what they send, one row each, "A = Space" (names as the phone's controller editor shows them: "LB", "LS click", "A long press", "↑"; what they send as the pad's frames show it, without "Key "; several mappings on one key share its row, "Space + Mouse R"). Touch buttons pressed on the phone show too, with only what they send ("Space", no name or "="); a touch Toggle that is on shows while it is on ("C on"), from the OVERLAY's latched bits. A new press takes the topmost free row and keeps it while held; let go, its row fades out over **Fade out** (Look; 0–2 s, 0.4 s; "At once" at 0) and leaves a gap; nothing moves up, and the next press fills the first gap (the same key pressed again while its row fades takes that row back). A row with a Toggle on stays while it is on, its text in the Toggles on colour ("C on"). A trigger's row has a thin bar along its foot for how far it is pulled. **Always shown** (Look; chips LS, RS, D-pad, A … RS click; default LS and RS) picks the controls that keep a fixed row on top, dim until something on them fires; the others show only while they fire. A stick's row (and the D-pad's while it is always shown) is "LS" with a pie: four slices (up, right, down, left) that light as their keys fire (a diagonal lights two) and, when it has Outer mappings, an outer ring of four parts that lights the same way (all four for an any-way Outer); symbols, not the real pie sizes; a dot shows where the stick points. Then its keys in W A S D order, its Outer keys, and what its axes move ("Mouse", "LS", "RS"), each bright while it fires. A stick that isn't always shown gets such a row only while something on it fires or, when it moves the mouse or a pad stick, while it is pushed; a control with no mappings has no row. An always-shown button's row joins all its mappings ("A = Space + E"). The window keeps the size set in Edit (new ones 240×220 px); rows grow with its width and rows that don't fit aren't drawn. Colours: Controller keys, What they send, Toggles on, Sticks at rest, Panel; **Transparent background** (Look, off) draws no Panel, only the keys. It shows only the attached controller (touch buttons are on the Phone overlay), never fades when the phone rests, and has no Turning tab. **Data** (no input cost): while a Keys overlay is shown the PC sets TEST_MODE OVERLAY_KEYS ([protocol](protocol.md)); the phone then adds a table of its attached pad's mappings in the set in use to the frames it sends (OVERLAY_FRAMES): each input (button, stick way, whole stick, trigger stage), its gesture, Outer and Toggle, its name and what it sends, with the sticks' and triggers' tuning and the tap time (`OverlayKeys.of`). The table is made again only when the workspace, the set in use or the attached controllers change (`GamepadRuntime`, on a background thread; the set in use comes from the pad screen, which already watches it), never from the engine. The PC works out what fires from the controller state each OVERLAY already carries (buttons, trigger pulls, sticks; `OverlayKeysReader`): held mappings, trigger stages and stick ways (half their travel, in their pie; Outer past the ring) with the phone's own thresholds; taps, double taps, presses and releases show for 150 ms, long presses once held past the tap time, and Toggles flip on each press; these last are worked out on the PC, so they can differ from the phone's in edge cases (a Toggle switched by something else, a tap that waits for a double tap). Touch buttons come from the OVERLAY's pressed and latched bitmaps and the table's touch part (each touch button's press, tap, double tap, long press and release mappings, by layout index; sticks, swipes and areas aren't shown). Buttons the OVERLAY doesn't carry (back paddles, extra keys) don't show. Nothing goes in input packets, nothing runs on an input thread and nothing reads the engine; with no Keys overlay shown nothing of it runs.
- **Pointer** (`LaserDrawing`, `PaintRenderer`, `GyroPhone.Host/LaserTrace.cs`): a see-through sphere seen from a fixed axonometric angle (30° round, 30° from above, no perspective, so a beam into the far side shows as a line, not a dot; **Axonometric view**, on by default, turned off shows it face-on instead; **Sphere turns, beam stays ahead**, off by default, keeps the beam pointing ahead (into the screen) and turns the sphere with its grid, axes and paint under it instead, the paint staying where it was painted on the sphere; **Sphere / Gun / Crosshair** look: Gun shows a shaded low-poly gun aiming as the beam does instead of the sphere, grid, beam and dot, Draw mode still painting where it points, best seen in the axonometric view; the gun is picked from Pistol, SMG, Assault rifle (default), Battle rifle, LMG, Shotgun and Sniper (`Guns`, as the phone's), each with its own model, recoil, default fire rate (400, 800, 600, 450, 700, 80, 45 a minute), muzzle size and calibre; **Lowered when idle** (on) rests it pitched 15° down, coming up over 120 ms while aiming or firing, and going back down as soon as both are let go, over Hip fire recovery; firing without aiming (hip fire) it comes up level and pulled back toward you by **Hip fire pull** (0–100%, 85%: the share of the way to its muzzle at the sphere's centre) and easing back over **Hip fire recovery** (0–2000 ms in 10 ms steps, 300 ms; 0 snaps back) from the moment fire is let go, the last kick playing out on top; the Gun look is drawn a little smaller where the fully hip-fired, kicked gun would otherwise reach past its frame (`LaserTrace.gunFrameScale`, measured at rest aiming ahead), easing over 120 ms; **Scope while aiming** (on, not on the pistol) grows a round scope onto it as you aim; **Fire display** and **Aim display** (each a row showing the picked button: a mouse button, controller button or LT/RT, or a key, or **None** to turn it off; clicking it opens a popup window in two tabs, Keyboard & mouse and Gamepad, with None on top, Esc closes it; `LaserFire`, default Mouse left and Mouse right): while GyroPhone sends the Fire display's button, Draw mode and Snake paint in the **Fire** to **Fire behind** colours with their own **Fire brush** (1–20°, 6°) and **Fire glow** (0–300%, 150%), and the Gun and Crosshair looks **kick** (**Kick** under Fire display; `CrosshairKick`, as the phone's) with the gun's recoil (the Crosshair look picks it under **Recoil like**): each shot the gun jumps back toward you with its muzzle up (`LaserTrace.RifleAt`), and the crosshair jumps out by **Kick size** (5–200%, 50%, each gun its own) and bounces back along one recoil curve baked once into a table (a 24 ms ease-out rise, then a damped spring that dips about a fifth under rest size and settles by 280 ms); **Off**, **Once** (default, one kick per press) or **Auto rifle** (kicks every shot at **Fire rate** 30–1200 a minute, each gun its own, while held, each kick rising from wherever the last one is); **Lines only** (default, crosshair only) grows the lines' length and gap, **Evenly, thickness too** also the line thickness and dot; **Muzzle flash** (on; `MuzzleFlash`, as the phone's) flashes on each shot in time with the kick (with Kick Off, once per press): white, cooling to the **Flash** colour (yellow) by 30 ms, then **Flash end** (orange) by 110 ms, and back by 220 ms; the crosshair takes those colours with a soft glow round its lines that fades with them, and the gun's muzzle blasts a flame plume along the barrel and four short slanting jets (each shot its own lengths and turn), as wide as its muzzle and as long as its calibre, with a white-hot core; under it on the Gun look, **Flash size** (25–300%, 100%, each gun its own) scales the whole blast; **Simulate spread** (off; Gun and Crosshair looks, and the Sphere look in Draw mode) lands each shot a little off the aim point, a random way (evenly over a circle) and up to the gun's **Spread** (0–10°, each gun its own: pistol 1.5°, SMG 2.5°, assault rifle 1.2°, battle rifle 1°, LMG 2°, shotgun 4°, sniper 0.3°); the crosshair and gun kick toward where it landed and spring back with the kick (`LaserTrace.shotPoint`, `turnToward`); **Bloom** (on) widens it for shots fired in a row, up to twice over 10 shots, settling once fire is let go for half a second (`CrosshairKick.State.bloom`). **Draw each shot** (off, Draw mode only) marks a hit where each shot lands (a shotgun its 8 pellets, at half size), in the Fire to Fire behind colours, as wide as the Fire brush, shaped by **Hit look**: **Dot** (default), **Ring**, **Cross** or **Bullet hole** (a dark centre in a bright rim) (`LaserTrace.hits`, the newest 2048 kept); hits never fade, they go with Clear, a ratchet clear or Clear displays; firing then paints no Fire line (aiming and the beam's own paint go on as set); without Simulate spread every hit lands on the aim point; on the Sphere look the shots come **Once** a press or by **Auto rifle** at the Fire rate of the gun picked under **Shoots like**. Every gun is shown scaled to fit, not to real size: as long as the assault rifle, but no taller than 1.4 times it (`Guns.Gun.fit`), so the pistol shows about 1.6 times its size. Then, while it sends the Aim display's (and not Fire's: firing wins), the paint takes the **Aim** to **Aim behind** colours with **Aim brush** (3°) and **Aim glow** (50%), the Crosshair eases (over 120 ms) down to **Crosshair while aiming** (20–100%, 75%) of its size and gap and to the **Crosshair aiming** colour (laser red), and the Rifle is shouldered (pulled 0.12 back toward you), a scope grows onto its carry rail and a thin **laser sight** in the Aim colour runs from its muzzle to a dot where it lands; the Sphere look doesn't change. A glow over 100% blooms: brighter, wider (its faint edge lifted, gl^(1/k)) and the line's core lit toward white; the paint keeps how much of each texel and of its glow is shots' and aiming's (`LaserPaint.fire`, `aim`, `glowFire`, `glowAim`), so each keeps its own colour and glow. The PC overlay also sees them pressed on the PC's controller with "Use PC controller for gyro ratchet" on; the phone display can't see those presses. **Paint only while firing or aiming** (off) paints only while one of them is sent; **Crosshair** shows only a crosshair lying on the sphere where the beam lands, facing its centre (so it squashes toward the edge), with the grid, axes and drawing as set: shape Cross, Cross with gap (default), Circle, Circle and cross, Dot or T (`LaserTrace.crosshair` / `CrosshairOn`), Size 2–30° (6°), Gap 0–10° (2°), Thickness 0.5–6 (2), Centre dot (on), and Outline (a dark edge, on); it turns about the beam only as the pose does (with Trackball, as the **Roll** row maps it, so None keeps it upright and Invert turns it the other way), coloured by the way the beam last moved (the **Moving** colours below; it keeps the last way's colour when the beam stops), dimmed while a ratchet is held), with its **15° grid** and its **axes** (the laser's own, turning with it: named as it aims, facing −Z into the screen: pitch X right red, yaw Y up green, roll Z along the beam blue, as in a normal map; both on by default) and, in the Sphere look only, **Gimbal rings** (off): yaw (green, outside), pitch (red) and roll (blue, innermost), one rigid set turning with the laser's axes, each round its own axis (`LaserTrace.gimbalRing` / `GimbalRing`), so each ring is the path the beam and crosshair take turning about it. With Trackball a None axis's ring is faint and dashed and its laser axis grey and dashed with a padlock at its tip (Camera's roll too), and an axis and its ring light up (thicker) while the gyro turns it (`GyroDisplayFeed.trackballActive` / `OverlayPose.Active`). So with the sphere still you see the laser's axes and rings turn, and with Sphere turns the sphere turns round the still beam and its rings. The laser at the centre aims as you aim the phone held as a gamepad, along its **roll axis** (out of its top edge; into the screen at rest, so a yaw turn swings it sideways and pitch up and down; with Turns with Camera, where the camera looks; with Trackball, **Laser faces** picks which of its directions it shines along, −Z (into the screen at rest, default), +Z, ±X or ±Y, so with the axis rows a trackball can match Phone or Camera): a beam to where it hits the inside, a dot there and a short **roll tick** pointing the way the phone's screen faces. The beam dims while a ratchet is held. **Perspective** (on): nearer parts of the sphere, its paint and the pointer look bigger. **Draw mode** (off): the beam paints the sphere's inside as you aim, **Brush** 1–20° wide (4°), and the paint stays until cleared. The paint is a map round the sphere (`LaserPaint`, 512, 1024 or 2048 texels round by **Paint detail** Low / Normal / High; changing it clears the paint) holding each texel's paint amount, Fire share and glow, premultiplied; a new stamp goes over what is there (a + b(1 − a)), so where lines cross the paint builds up and their colours blend, while within one line the strongest stamp wins (even along its length). **Snake** (off, with or without Draw mode): a line following the beam along the newest **Length** (10–720°, 180°) of its path; only that stretch of points is kept. **Zip back** (as the phone's), two switches that can both be on: **Continuous** (off): the tail always runs toward the head at **Zip speed** (1–2000 °/s, 360, in 1 °/s steps, finer toward the slow end), while the beam moves and while it holds still, so moving faster than that the Snake grows (up to its Length) and slower it shrinks; **On ratchet** (on): a ratchet press runs the tail back into the head, the whole Snake in **Zip back in** (0.05–2 s, 0.4 s), on top of Continuous. A held ratchet doesn't stop it: while it runs the trace is fed by the clock, not just new poses. Zipped all the way it stays as a dot the size of its head, never gone. It grows again from its head as the beam moves on (`LaserTrace.ZipBack`). It has its own look, as on the phone: **Colour by**, **Facing shade**, **Inside**, **Light** and **Shine** under Snake, starting from the paint's until changed (`OverlaySettings.Snake*`), and its own Fire and Aim brushes. A pointer that paints or has a Snake follows every gyro sample: the phone sends each sample's turn (OVERLAY_SAMPLES, [protocol](protocol.md)) just before each OVERLAY while the PC asks, the PC turns the pose sample by sample (`OverlayPose.FeedSamples`, `PoseHistory`), and the window feeds the trace every pose, so a fast turn paints a smooth curve, as the phone's does. **Snake brush** (1–20°, 4°, its own; Draw mode's **Brush** shows only with Draw mode on; shots and aiming keep the Fire and Aim brushes) wide at its head, thinning by **Taper** (0–100%, 80%: the tail a fifth of the head; 0 keeps one width) and fading by **Tail fade** (0–100%, 50%) toward its tail, with a soft **Snake glow** (0–100%, off). It runs along a smooth curve through the path's points (`LaserTrace.Curve`, a centripetal Catmull-Rom spline on the sphere, which never loops or overshoots), cut into steps of at most 0.5° with its colour, width and fade blending along each, so a fast turn still draws round with no corners or visible joints; each pixel takes the strongest piece over it (and its glow the strongest glow), so crossings don't darken. As the phone's. The PC draws the paint and Snake on the **graphics card** (`GpuPaint`, Direct3D 11, shader model 5, feature level 11): the map lives on the card as textures and only the rows of its tiles changed since the last frame go up (`LaserPaint.TileVersion`); a pixel shader (the phone's PointerPaintShader and `PaintPicture`'s maths) finds each pixel's point of the sphere, reads the map there smoothly and shades it, into a picture per half the same size as the CPU's below, drawn again only when the paint, view or look changed, so a turning sphere costs the CPU nothing. The Snake's pieces are worked out on the CPU as before and the card draws them as round-ended strips into a line picture and a glow picture per kind (moving, aiming, firing), each pixel keeping its strongest piece and its strongest glow (a max blend); Direct2D then draws the paint, then each kind's glow and line at that kind's own colours' opacity (moving, then aiming, then firing on top, as the phone; the CPU path does the same). The picture matches the CPU's except where differently coloured Snake pieces cross (each colour channel keeps its own strongest). Where the card can't (an older card, no shader compiler `d3dcompiler_47`, a refused call), that device draws them on the CPU from then on, as follows. On the CPU (`PaintRenderer`) they go into a small premultiplied picture per half (up to 160, 256 or 420 pixels across by Paint detail; `GyroPhone.Host/PaintPicture.cs`), each pixel finding the point of the sphere it shows (in perspective or flat) and reading the map there smoothly, then scaled up smoothly by the canvas (`ICanvas.DrawPixels`). What stays the same from frame to frame is kept in tables: each pixel's point on the sphere, facing shade, light, highlight and scene colour (made again when the size, view, perspective, light or Moving colours change) and the four texels it reads with their weights (made again when the sphere turns too), so a frame only reads the map and mixes colours. The map keeps track of which of its 32 × 32 texel tiles each stamp changed (`LaserPaint.TileVersion`), so a new stamp redraws only the pixels that read those tiles, and only on the half that shows them; a changed view, size or look, a turning sphere, a cleared map or a large change draws the whole half again (spread over the cores). Snake is drawn over a copy of the paint whenever it or the paint changed. The picture is exactly the same as drawing every pixel each time. The **Paint** settings: **Colour by** **Where it is** (default: the paint uncovers the sphere's own colours, the Moving colours laid on it as a normal map by where it lands, fixed to the scene so they don't turn with a turning sphere) or **The way it moved** (each sweep's direction colour, kept per texel); shots take the **Fire** to **Fire behind** colours, aiming the **Aim** to **Aim behind** colours; with Draw mode **Softness** (50 %, the brush's edge; shapes new strokes only), **Glow** (50 %) and **Outline** (off, the **Paint outline** colour); **Facing shade** (50 %, darker where the surface turns away), **Inside** (70 %, the far half seen from inside), and a **Light** fixed in the scene (it stays put however the sphere or pointer turns): **Light strength** (50 %), from **Upper left** (default) / **Above** / **Upper right** / **Front**, and **Shine** (30 %, a glossy highlight); **Line smoothing** steadies the painted line against hand shake. A held ratchet stops painting. **Clear** wipes the paint and Snake, and so does turning both Draw mode and Snake off, or a ratchet press: Draw mode's **Clear on ratchet** wipes the paint and Snake's **Clear on ratchet** (under Snake) wipes the Snake, each off by default; a **Clear displays** mapping (Other targets) clears every pointer on the pad and the PC at once (the PC tray menu also has **Clear pointers**). Paint is not saved. In Fixed pointer mode (Sphere turns) with Trackball, the sphere turns by the trackball's pose and **Laser faces** still sets where the beam points. **Way colours** (paint and crosshair, `LaserTrace.DirectionColor`): the Colours list has **Moving right / left / up / down / toward you / away**, by default a normal map's (pink, teal, light green, purple, blue, olive); a slanted move blends them. **Smooth way colours** (on) takes each stroke's way over ±2° of its line and eases the crosshair's over 60 ms, so small shakes don't flicker.

The phone overlay is only drawn on the PC; the phone sends its raw motion, layout, touches and presses (OVERLAY, OVERLAY_LAYOUT) only while some overlay is shown (HOST_STATUS flags tell it). The PC makes the pose itself (`GyroPhone.Host/OverlayPose.cs`, a port of the phone's `GyroPose`).

## Per-window settings

Overlays page: the switches for all (Edit, Hide all, Level all), the list of windows (Show each, Add, shortcuts), and the picked window's settings: its **label** in a box on top (`OverlaySettings.Label`; empty, the box shows the name it has without one greyed: its kind's name, numbered when there are several of that kind), then tabs, grouped as on the phone: Window, Look, Turning, and for the pointer the phone's own layout: **Look** (axonometric, perspective, Fixed pointer mode, grid, axes, Fire display with its kick, recoil, muzzle flash, Shoots like and spread, Aim display, gimbal rings), **Paint** (Draw mode with its brush and Clear on ratchet; Shots and aiming; the paint's look; Paint and Snake: line smoothing, Paint only while firing or aiming; Clear), **Snake** (Snake with its length, brush, taper, tail fade, glow and Clear on ratchet; Zip back; its Fire and Aim brushes; its look), **Colours** and **Turning** (on a trackball, **Pointer faces** sits under the axis rows). Each setting sits right under the switch it belongs to. Other overlays keep their colours in Look; the FPS overlay, Ratchet ring, Speed graph and Keys overlay have no Turning. A tab that goes away (another overlay picked) falls back to Look.

- **Window**: **Show this overlay**; **Behind other windows** (off by default; `OverlaySettings.BehindWindows`): the window is not topmost and goes to the bottom of the z-order, so a game covers it and it isn't seen while playing, while OBS Window Capture with the "Windows 10 (1903 and up)" method still records it (that capture doesn't need the window uncovered, only not minimized; the overlay keeps drawing as usual). In Edit it comes up on top to be placed, and goes back behind when Edit is turned off. **All behind** at the top, beside Edit and Hide all, turns it on for every overlay, or off for every one when they all have it; it shows on only while every overlay has it; also in the tray menu and on **Ctrl+Alt+B**. Place and size (default 420 × 300), opacity (0.1–1), background **Transparent** or **Green** (for an OBS chroma key), **Quality** High (default) / Balanced / Fast, **Frame rate** every screen refresh / about 60 (default) / about 30: a cap draws every Nth refresh of the screen the window is on, N = refresh ÷ cap rounded, at least 1 (60 → 72 fps at 144 Hz, 55 at 165 Hz, 60 at 120 or 60 Hz), so pictures stay tied to the screen.
- **Look**: kind-specific options above, colours (one swatch per colour; Windows colour picker), finger dots (off) and trails (on).
- **Turning**: **Turns with**: **Phone** (default: how the phone is held), **Camera** (where the game's camera points, turning as a game camera does: left and right about the vertical and up and down about its own right, pitch within ±89°, never rolling; the gyro axis that moves the mouse sideways turns it left and right and the one on mouse Y up and down (`GyroDisplayFeed.cameraMapOf`, sent to the PC in OVERLAY bytes 149 and 150), with every other camera move, touchpad, buttons, controllers, keys, glides; a ratchet stops it as it stops the camera) or **Trackball** (turned by the gyro and every other camera move: for each of its axes, Yaw (Y, up: turning left and right), Pitch (X, right) and Roll (Z, along the laser's beam, through the screen at rest), you pick which gyro turn turns it (Yaw / Pitch / Roll / None) and can invert it; **None** holds that axis at 0 whatever the others do, as Camera never rolls (saved Lock flags from older versions read as None). **Turns about**: **The sphere's axes** (default) turns it as a gimbal, left and right about the sphere's vertical, then up and down about its own right, then about where it faces (`GyroPose.gimbalTurn`, `OverlayPose.GimbalTurn`), and each row has **Stop at ±90°**: Pitch (on by default) stops at straight up or down (±89°, the poles), and without it goes over the top with left and right staying as seen; Yaw and Roll (off by default) stop once turned sideways. **Its own axes** turns it about its own axes as it now points (left and right about its own up, up and down about its own right, `GyroPose.turnOwn`, `OverlayPose.Turn`), so the crosshair moves at right angles as it sees it and never rolls by itself; there are no poles, so Stop at ±90° is hidden. Moving in a loop leaves it a little rolled (the sphere's curvature: a loop's twist equals its area), so picking it also turns on Roll's ease back When the phone stops, which levels that out. By default it turns as Camera does with the default gyro mouse: the gyro's yaw turns it left and right, pitch up and down (stopping at the poles), Roll None, about the sphere's axes, and **As the camera** puts that back); Camera and Trackball turn as far as the game's camera (when its turn is known) or Spin 0.1–10× times the phone, have no Gravity, Compass or Turn amount, and Level sets them straight; a Trackball has no overall Ease back: instead each axis row has **Ease <axis> axis back** with its own options (On ratchet or When the phone stops, Delay, By time or By speed; default off), easing just that axis to straight (`GyroDisplay.trackballEase`, `OverlaySettings.TrackballEase`). **Level** (the way the phone is held now shows face-on; with Gravity only the tilt is kept), **Gravity** (tilt held to gravity, default on), **Compass** (heading held by the magnetometer, off), **Ease** back to straight (on): after 300 ms still, over 2 s or at a speed (90 °/s); **Ease on ratchet** (on: while a ratchet is held it eases back until straight, instead of whenever the phone stops); heading only (without gravity). **Amount**: **Turn amount** (0.5–3×, not on a trackball, which turns by Spin) and **Level**.

The phone's own gyro displays use the same Look and Turning settings, names and defaults (see [Pad UI](phone-pad-ui.md#gyro-displays)); their values are separate.

**Save as preset** (above Remove) saves the picked overlay as a preset named by its label (else its kind's name): every setting and the window's size, not its place (`HostSettings.OverlayPresets`). Presets show under **Add overlay** after the kinds with a ★; clicking one opens a new window from it where a new overlay opens; right-click one to **Rename…** or **Delete** it. The phone's presets are separate.

An overlay's label names it in the list, the tray menu's **Show** items, the Remove question and the caption at its bottom edge while Edit is on; the overlay never draws it otherwise.

**Shortcuts** (global, `Hotkeys`, a hidden message window so they work with no overlay window shown; held only while at least one overlay is set up, so a PC without overlays keeps every key; reconfigurable, one press never does two things): **Ctrl+Alt+R** Level all, **Ctrl+Alt+O** Hide all, **Ctrl+Alt+E** Edit, **Ctrl+Alt+B** All behind. A Ctrl+Alt shortcut whose key types a character with AltGr in any of the PC's keyboard layouts (AltGr is Ctrl+Alt to Windows: €, ę, ó) is not held, and its field says it is taken, so typing that character keeps working. A **Set orientation** mapping on the phone (or its Set orientation button) starts every overlay shown again from the phone's new base orientation: its saved Level grip cleared, facing ahead, Fade when phone rests counting from the way the phone is held then (OVERLAY byte 139 counts its presses; a change starts them over, the first count seen in a session does nothing). In the phone's World and Player gyro space (OVERLAY flag 128) overlays show the phone's real pose against gravity and take no saved grip. A **Clear displays** mapping clears every laser pointer overlay the same way (OVERLAY byte 148).

**Fade when phone rests** (a card under the shortcuts, for every overlay; `OverlayRest`, `HostSettings.OverlayRestFade`; on by default): an overlay fades while the phone lies on a table. It rests for an overlay once, for the whole **Quiet for** time (3 s, 0.5–30 s), the phone has lain within **Flat within** (10°, 1–45°) of the tilt that overlay was last leveled at (Level, Level all or Set orientation save which way was up, `OverlaySettings.LevelUp`, with or without Gravity; an overlay leveled before this reads it from its resting grip, one never leveled never fades), turned slower than **Still below** (0.5 °/s, 0.1–5, measured per OVERLAY; the card shows the phone's speed now, "still" or "moving"), read about 1 g on the accelerometer, and had no finger, pressed control, ratchet, stick off centre or attached controller's button, trigger or stick in use (a latched control is a state and doesn't count). A frame without gyro counts as moving, so a held phone (which shakes at 1–3 °/s) never rests. It then fades to **Fade to** (30 %) of its own opacity over **Fade over** (0.5 s) and comes back at once on any input or movement. **Also when the phone stops sending** (on): no OVERLAY for the quiet time (screen off, app closed, link stopped) fades them too. Never in Edit. A swap-chain window only changes its constant alpha (free); a copied one draws again.

## Edit and click-through

Overlay windows (`OverlayForm`) are borderless, always on top, per-pixel-alpha layered windows that never take focus. The mouse passes through them, except in **Edit** (one switch for all, not saved: every start begins with it off): then they can be dragged and resized by their edges, the mouse wheel over a window picks it and resizes it, keeping its shape and centre (10 % a notch, 2 % with **Shift** held, never below 120×90; the window's own wheel messages, which Windows sends to the window under the pointer with "Scroll inactive windows" on, its default; no mouse hook, so the wheel anywhere else reaches the window under it as usual), and a click without a drag picks a window, cycling through overlapping ones. A double-click opens the companion window on the Overlays page with that window picked, or brings it to the front if it is already open (`TrayApp.OverlayClicked`). Each has an owner window rather than the tool-window style, so **OBS Window Capture** can record it, over games running windowed or borderless. Its title, which OBS finds the window by, is "GyroPhone Companion <kind> overlay", then ": <label>" when it has one (" (2)" and so on added when two of a kind share a label), or ": <number>" among its kind when there are several unlabelled ones; it follows the saved list order, so it is the same after a restart and OBS finds the same window again. Rename a window's label and its OBS source needs picking again.

## Drawing

- **GPU**: one shared Direct3D 11 / Direct2D / DirectWrite device (`D2DCanvas`), presented by a swap chain without a CPU copy: first a composition swap chain (flip model, two buffers, premultiplied alpha, `CreateSwapChainForComposition` shown by a DirectComposition visual on a window made without a redirection surface, `WS_EX_NOREDIRECTIONBITMAP`), which the desktop compositor shows as is, so a picture reaches the screen about one refresh sooner than through the copy-model swap chain, whose pictures the compositor first copies into the window's redirection surface ("Composed: Copy with GPU GDI" in PresentMon). When the composition swap chain fails, the window is made again with a redirection surface for the copy-model one (constant window alpha, frame extended over it all), and the composition one is tried again 10 minutes later. Diagnostics' overlay draw lines say "GPU, composed", "GPU" (copy model), "GPU, copied" or "CPU". At most one picture per swap chain waiting for the graphics card (the device's frame latency set to the number of swap chains, 1–3; not 1 for the device, as the windows present one after another and each would wait for the one before), so on a busy graphics card an overlay drops behind by a frame at most instead of three; a lost device (sleep, driver update) is replaced. When making a swap chain fails, the windows copy their pictures through the CPU (UpdateLayeredWindow) for a while and then try it again: 2 s after a failure, 5 minutes after 3 in a row, one window at a time once its copied picture has drawn, the others following once it works. **CPU fallback** (GDI) when Direct2D can't start. **3D on the card** (`Gpu3D`, Direct3D 11, shader model 5): the 3D shape overlay goes up to the card once per shape and look (its lines as pieces with their ends, an edge also with its two faces' normals; its faces as triangles), and each frame sends only its turn; the card turns them, tells front from back (a line piece per pixel by which side of the middle plane it is on, an edge by whether either face faces you), shades faces by how much they face you, and draws round-ended lines with smooth edges. Each line layer (hidden lines, front lines, the rim, the equator, each gimbal ring) is drawn with a max blend, so a line's joins don't double up, then laid over the picture at its colour's opacity; the ball's fill is Direct2D's radial gradient worked out per pixel; a shape that can hide its own faces (filled torus, camera, plane) uses a depth buffer, so edges behind nearer faces are hidden. The picture is drawn again only when the turn, size or look changes, and Direct2D draws it in one go. Where the card can't, the shape is drawn on the CPU as before. The pointer's 15° grid and its gimbal rings (all but held, dashed ones) go up the same way and turn on the card with the view, the sphere's turn and perspective. The gun look's faces are worked out on the CPU each frame as before (a few dozen, cheap) and the card fills and outlines them in drawing order (each face after every face it hides part of, compared where each two overlap, and faces turned away from the camera left out as it sees them in perspective; `LaserTrace.DrawOrder`, as the phone's) (flat items: filled polygons and round-ended, smooth-edged lines drawn in order; the pieces of one outline or polyline share each pixel round their joins, the piece before taking it when both are as near, so a see-through outline, such as a handle button's, is one even line with no brighter dot at each corner, as Direct2D's one stroke), which was the costly part. The 3D phone's body, sides, back and camera island and its controller handles are worked out on the CPU as before and drawn by the card the same way; its screen (the layout's controls, labels, stick knobs, finger dots and trails, notes) is drawn flat by Direct2D into a picture on the card about 1.25 times as fine as it shows face on, only when what is on it changes (a press, a stick, a finger, a fading trail, the layout or colours), and the card lays that picture on the turned screen in perspective, reading it from smaller copies of itself (each half the one before, made each time it is drawn) by how much it is shrunk each way (anisotropic filtering), so its 1-pixel outlines stay whole lines instead of breaking into dots when it shows smaller or tilted; tilted far over its labels are a little softer than the CPU's. Each picture is drawn at four samples a pixel (multisampling) and then averaged, so the edges of filled shapes and faces (the phone's body, the gun, a shape's faces) are smooth, as Direct2D's. Each of these is its own picture (shape, phone, gun, grid near and far, rings near and far), drawn by Direct2D at its place in the order. The overlay timing log says "3D on GPU" or "3D on CPU (why)".
- **Pacer** (`OverlayPacer`): a background thread waits for each compositor refresh (DwmFlush), spaced by at least the refresh period, and draws there, not on the UI thread, also in Edit (moving a window needs no new picture). The UI thread draws a window only right after its size changed (its swap chain is resized there, at most once a frame however fast an edge is dragged), and a copied window in Edit (its picture would race the move and resize there); a settings change (a slider step) is drawn by the pacer at its next tick, whatever the window's frame cap. It sleeps while no overlay is open. Each window draws at most at its frame rate. The windows read the engine's published overlay view and the PC controller's own copy, never the engine lock, so drawing can't hold up input ([threads](pc-companion.md#threads-and-timing)). The pacer ticks at the fastest screen's refresh rate among the screens the open windows are on (each window reads its own screen's rate every 2 s), every Nth refresh for the fastest window's cap, sleeping through the refreshes it skips and still waiting for the compositor (DwmFlush) before the one it draws at. While no window has been busy for a second (no new phone frame, nothing moving, fading or changed, as with the phone off or asleep), it ticks only 20 times a second (`OverlayPacing.IdleTickMs`), enough for the FPS overlay's 4 pictures; new phone data or a settings change brings it back to every refresh at once.
- **Only what changed**: a window draws again when a new phone frame shows something new: what it draws besides the pose (fingers, presses, latches, sticks, layout, flags, the controller) differs from the last picture, or the pose turned 0.25 px or more at the window's size (its longer side, times the turn amount), so a still phone's gyro noise draws nothing. Also when the layout, trails, the laser pointer's trail, paint, fire or aim, Edit or the settings change, and every 500 ms for the status (not while it shows no phone and that hasn't changed). Faded out by Fade when phone rests, it draws at most 4 times a second, until the rest ends.
- Draw times are logged every 30 s. In game the overlays cost about a fifth of one CPU core.
