# PC: overlays

Windows on the PC that show the phone's motion over a game or in a stream.

## Windows and kinds

Any number of overlay windows (`OverlaySettings`, Overlays page › Add), each one of:

- **Phone**: the phone in 3D with the workspace's layout, presses and fingers on its screen; optional Switch-style **controller handles** on its sides (narrow, sticks and buttons centred, −/+/Home next to the phone edge, bumper/trigger pills on the front, presses shown visually, no labels). Handles: **While a controller is attached** (default) / Always / Off; **Controller**: Phone (default) or PC (the PC's controller, only while the phone's PC ratchet switch is on).
- **3D shape**: Globe (default), Icosphere (subdivision 0–3), Cube, Tetrahedron, Dodecahedron, Torus, Gimbal rings, Paper plane, Camera (seen from behind, lens pointing where it looks); smooth edges, hidden edges, filled faces, equator, screen and top marks; **Axonometric view** (off).
- **Axonometric view** (Phone and 3D shape, off by default): seen from the laser pointer's fixed angle (30° round, 30° from above) instead of face-on, with the world's axes (yaw toward you red, pitch right green, roll up blue, as the gimbal's rings) through it; the drawing shrinks a little so the axes fit.
- **Attitude indicator**: horizon with a heading tape.
- **Bubble disk**: heading disk with a tilt dot (range 30°), crosshair.
- **Pointer** (`LaserDrawing`, `PaintRenderer`, `GyroPhone.Host/LaserTrace.cs`): a see-through sphere seen from a fixed axonometric angle (30° round, 30° from above, no perspective, so a beam into the far side shows as a line, not a dot; **Axonometric view**, on by default, turned off shows it face-on instead; **Sphere turns, beam stays ahead**, off by default, keeps the beam pointing ahead (into the screen) and turns the sphere with its grid, axes and paint under it instead, the paint staying where it was painted on the sphere; **Sphere / Gun / Crosshair** look: Gun shows a shaded low-poly gun aiming as the beam does instead of the sphere, grid, beam and dot, Draw mode still painting where it points, best seen in the axonometric view; the gun is picked from Pistol, SMG, Assault rifle (default), Battle rifle, LMG, Shotgun and Sniper (`Guns`, as the phone's), each with its own model, recoil, default fire rate (400, 800, 600, 450, 700, 80, 45 a minute), muzzle size and calibre; **Lowered when idle** (on) rests it pitched 15° down, coming up over 120 ms while aiming or firing; firing without aiming (hip fire) it comes up level and pulled back toward you by **Hip fire pull** (0–100%, 85%: the share of the way to its muzzle at the sphere's centre) and easing back over **Hip fire recovery** (0–2000 ms in 10 ms steps, 300 ms; 0 snaps back) from the moment fire is let go, the last kick playing out on top; the Gun look is drawn a little smaller where the fully hip-fired, kicked gun would otherwise reach past its frame (`LaserTrace.gunFrameScale`, measured at rest aiming ahead), easing over 120 ms; **Scope while aiming** (on, not on the pistol) grows a round scope onto it as you aim; **Fire display** and **Aim display** (each a row showing the picked button: a mouse button, controller button or LT/RT, or a key, or **None** to turn it off; clicking it opens a popup window in two tabs, Keyboard & mouse and Gamepad, with None on top, Esc closes it; `LaserFire`, default Mouse left and Mouse right): while GyroPhone sends the Fire display's button, Draw mode and Snake paint in the **Fire** to **Fire behind** colours with their own **Fire brush** (1–20°, 6°) and **Fire glow** (0–300%, 150%), and the Gun and Crosshair looks **kick** (**Kick** under Fire display; `CrosshairKick`, as the phone's) with the gun's recoil (the Crosshair look picks it under **Recoil like**): each shot the gun jumps back toward you with its muzzle up (`LaserTrace.RifleAt`), and the crosshair jumps out by **Kick size** (5–200%, 50%, each gun its own) and bounces back along one recoil curve baked once into a table (a 24 ms ease-out rise, then a damped spring that dips about a fifth under rest size and settles by 280 ms); **Off**, **Once** (default, one kick per press) or **Auto rifle** (kicks every shot at **Fire rate** 30–1200 a minute, each gun its own, while held, each kick rising from wherever the last one is); **Lines only** (default, crosshair only) grows the lines' length and gap, **Evenly, thickness too** also the line thickness and dot; **Muzzle flash** (on; `MuzzleFlash`, as the phone's) flashes on each shot in time with the kick (with Kick Off, once per press): white, cooling to the **Flash** colour (yellow) by 30 ms, then **Flash end** (orange) by 110 ms, and back by 220 ms; the crosshair takes those colours with a soft glow round its lines that fades with them, and the gun's muzzle blasts a flame plume along the barrel and four short slanting jets (each shot its own lengths and turn), as wide as its muzzle and as long as its calibre, with a white-hot core; **Simulate spread** (off; Gun and Crosshair looks, and the Sphere look in Draw mode) lands each shot a little off the aim point, a random way (evenly over a circle) and up to the gun's **Spread** (0–10°, each gun its own: pistol 1.5°, SMG 2.5°, assault rifle 1.2°, battle rifle 1°, LMG 2°, shotgun 4°, sniper 0.3°); the crosshair and gun kick toward where it landed and spring back with the kick (`LaserTrace.shotPoint`, `turnToward`); **Bloom** (on) widens it for shots fired in a row, up to twice over 10 shots, settling once fire is let go for half a second (`CrosshairKick.State.bloom`). **Draw each shot** (off, Draw mode only) marks a hit where each shot lands (a shotgun its 8 pellets, at half size), in the Fire to Fire behind colours, as wide as the Fire brush, shaped by **Hit look**: **Dot** (default), **Ring**, **Cross** or **Bullet hole** (a dark centre in a bright rim) (`LaserTrace.hits`, the newest 2048 kept); hits never fade, they go with Clear, a ratchet clear or Clear displays; firing then paints no Fire line (aiming and the beam's own paint go on as set); without Simulate spread every hit lands on the aim point; on the Sphere look the shots come **Once** a press or by **Auto rifle** at the Fire rate of the gun picked under **Shoots like**. Every gun is shown scaled to fit, not to real size: as long as the assault rifle, but no taller than 1.4 times it (`Guns.Gun.fit`), so the pistol shows about 1.6 times its size. Then, while it sends the Aim display's (and not Fire's: firing wins), the paint takes the **Aim** to **Aim behind** colours with **Aim brush** (3°) and **Aim glow** (50%), the Crosshair eases (over 120 ms) down to **Crosshair while aiming** (20–100%, 75%) of its size and gap and to the **Crosshair aiming** colour (laser red), and the Rifle is shouldered (pulled 0.12 back toward you), a scope grows onto its carry rail and a thin **laser sight** in the Aim colour runs from its muzzle to a dot where it lands; the Sphere look doesn't change. A glow over 100% blooms: brighter, wider (its faint edge lifted, gl^(1/k)) and the line's core lit toward white; the paint keeps how much of each texel and of its glow is shots' and aiming's (`LaserPaint.fire`, `aim`, `glowFire`, `glowAim`), so each keeps its own colour and glow. The PC overlay also sees them pressed on the PC's controller with "Use PC controller for gyro ratchet" on; the phone display can't see those presses. **Paint only while firing or aiming** (off) paints only while one of them is sent; **Crosshair** shows only a crosshair lying on the sphere where the beam lands, facing its centre (so it squashes toward the edge), with the grid, axes and drawing as set: shape Cross, Cross with gap (default), Circle, Circle and cross, Dot or T (`LaserTrace.crosshair` / `CrosshairOn`), Size 2–30° (6°), Gap 0–10° (2°), Thickness 0.5–6 (2), Centre dot (on), and Outline (a dark edge, on); it turns about the beam only as the pose does (with Trackball, as the **Roll** row maps it, so None keeps it upright and Invert turns it the other way), coloured by the way the beam last moved (the **Moving** colours below; it keeps the last way's colour when the beam stops), dimmed while a ratchet is held), with its **15° grid** and its **axes** (the laser's own, turning with it: named as it aims, facing −Z into the screen: pitch X right red, yaw Y up green, roll Z along the beam blue, as in a normal map; both on by default) and, in the Sphere look only, **Gimbal rings** (off): yaw (green, outside), pitch (red) and roll (blue, innermost), one rigid set turning with the laser's axes, each round its own axis (`LaserTrace.gimbalRing` / `GimbalRing`), so each ring is the path the beam and crosshair take turning about it. With Trackball a None axis's ring is faint and dashed and its laser axis grey and dashed with a padlock at its tip (Camera's roll too), and an axis and its ring light up (thicker) while the gyro turns it (`GyroDisplayFeed.trackballActive` / `OverlayPose.Active`). So with the sphere still you see the laser's axes and rings turn, and with Sphere turns the sphere turns round the still beam and its rings. The laser at the centre aims as you aim the phone held as a gamepad, along its **roll axis** (out of its top edge; into the screen at rest, so a yaw turn swings it sideways and pitch up and down; with Turns with Camera, where the camera looks; with Trackball, **Laser faces** picks which of its directions it shines along, −Z (into the screen at rest, default), +Z, ±X or ±Y, so with the axis rows a trackball can match Phone or Camera): a beam to where it hits the inside, a dot there and a short **roll tick** pointing the way the phone's screen faces. The beam dims while a ratchet is held. **Perspective** (on): nearer parts of the sphere, its paint and the pointer look bigger. **Draw mode** (off): the beam paints the sphere's inside as you aim, **Brush** 1–20° wide (4°), and the paint stays until cleared. The paint is a map round the sphere (`LaserPaint`, 512, 1024 or 2048 texels round by **Paint detail** Low / Normal / High; changing it clears the paint) holding each texel's paint amount, Fire share and glow, premultiplied; a new stamp goes over what is there (a + b(1 − a)), so where lines cross the paint builds up and their colours blend, while within one line the strongest stamp wins (even along its length). **Snake** (off, with or without Draw mode): a line following the beam along the newest **Length** (10–720°, 180°) of its path; only that stretch of points is kept. **Snake brush** (1–20°, 4°, its own; Draw mode's **Brush** shows only with Draw mode on; shots and aiming keep the Fire and Aim brushes) wide at its head, thinning by **Taper** (0–100%, 80%: the tail a fifth of the head; 0 keeps one width) and fading by **Tail fade** (0–100%, 50%) toward its tail, with a soft **Snake glow** (0–100%, off). It runs along a smooth curve through the path's points (`LaserTrace.Curve`, a centripetal Catmull-Rom spline on the sphere, which never loops or overshoots), cut into steps of at most 0.5° with its colour, width and fade blending along each, so a fast turn still draws round with no corners or visible joints; each pixel takes the strongest piece over it (and its glow the strongest glow), so crossings don't darken. As the phone's. The PC draws the paint and Snake (`PaintRenderer`) on the CPU into a small premultiplied picture per half (up to 160, 256 or 420 pixels across by Paint detail; `GyroPhone.Host/PaintPicture.cs`), each pixel finding the point of the sphere it shows (in perspective or flat) and reading the map there smoothly, then scaled up smoothly by the canvas (`ICanvas.DrawPixels`). What stays the same from frame to frame is kept in tables: each pixel's point on the sphere, facing shade, light, highlight and scene colour (made again when the size, view, perspective, light or Moving colours change) and the four texels it reads with their weights (made again when the sphere turns too), so a frame only reads the map and mixes colours. The map keeps track of which of its 32 × 32 texel tiles each stamp changed (`LaserPaint.TileVersion`), so a new stamp redraws only the pixels that read those tiles, and only on the half that shows them; a changed view, size or look, a turning sphere, a cleared map or a large change draws the whole half again (spread over the cores). Snake is drawn over a copy of the paint whenever it or the paint changed. The picture is exactly the same as drawing every pixel each time. The **Paint** settings: **Colour by** **Where it is** (default: the paint uncovers the sphere's own colours, the Moving colours laid on it as a normal map by where it lands, fixed to the scene so they don't turn with a turning sphere) or **The way it moved** (each sweep's direction colour, kept per texel); shots take the **Fire** to **Fire behind** colours, aiming the **Aim** to **Aim behind** colours; with Draw mode **Softness** (50 %, the brush's edge; shapes new strokes only), **Glow** (50 %) and **Outline** (off, the **Paint outline** colour); **Facing shade** (50 %, darker where the surface turns away), **Inside** (70 %, the far half seen from inside), and a **Light** fixed in the scene (it stays put however the sphere or pointer turns): **Light strength** (50 %), from **Upper left** (default) / **Above** / **Upper right** / **Front**, and **Shine** (30 %, a glossy highlight); **Line smoothing** steadies the painted line against hand shake. A held ratchet stops painting. **Clear** wipes the paint and Snake, and so does turning both Draw mode and Snake off, or a ratchet press with **Clear on ratchet** on (off by default); a **Clear displays** mapping (Other targets) clears every pointer on the pad and the PC at once (the PC tray menu also has **Clear pointers**). Paint is not saved. In Fixed pointer mode (Sphere turns) with Trackball, the sphere turns by the trackball's pose and **Laser faces** still sets where the beam points. **Way colours** (paint and crosshair, `LaserTrace.DirectionColor`): the Colours list has **Moving right / left / up / down / toward you / away**, by default a normal map's (pink, teal, light green, purple, blue, olive); a slanted move blends them. **Smooth way colours** (on) takes each stroke's way over ±2° of its line and eases the crosshair's over 60 ms, so small shakes don't flicker.

The phone overlay is only drawn on the PC; the phone sends its raw motion, layout, touches and presses (OVERLAY, OVERLAY_LAYOUT) only while some overlay is shown (HOST_STATUS flags tell it). The PC makes the pose itself (`GyroPhone.Host/OverlayPose.cs`, a port of the phone's `GyroPose`).

## Per-window settings

Overlays page: the switches for all (Edit, Hide all, Level all), the list of windows (Show each, Add, shortcuts), and the picked window's settings: its **label** in a box on top (`OverlaySettings.Label`; empty, the box shows the name it has without one greyed: its kind's name, numbered when there are several of that kind), then tabs, grouped as on the phone: Window, Look, Turning, and for the pointer also Draw (Draw mode, Snake, brushes, Clear), Paint (shown while Draw mode or Snake is on) and Colours (its colours and Smooth way colours) between Look and Turning. Other overlays keep their colours in Look. A tab that goes away (another overlay picked, Draw mode and Snake off) falls back to Look.

- **Window**: place and size (default 420 × 300), opacity (0.1–1), background **Transparent** or **Green** (for an OBS chroma key), **Quality** High (default) / Balanced / Fast, **Frame rate** every screen refresh / about 60 (default) / about 30: a cap draws every Nth refresh of the screen the window is on, N = refresh ÷ cap rounded, at least 1 (60 → 72 fps at 144 Hz, 55 at 165 Hz, 60 at 120 or 60 Hz), so pictures stay tied to the screen.
- **Look**: kind-specific options above, colours (one swatch per colour; Windows colour picker), finger dots (off) and trails (on).
- **Turning**: **Turns with**: **Phone** (default: how the phone is held), **Camera** (where the game's camera points, turning as a game camera does: left and right about the vertical and up and down about its own right, pitch within ±89°, never rolling; the gyro axis that moves the mouse sideways turns it left and right and the one on mouse Y up and down (`GyroDisplayFeed.cameraMapOf`, sent to the PC in OVERLAY bytes 149 and 150), with every other camera move, touchpad, buttons, controllers, keys, glides; a ratchet stops it as it stops the camera) or **Trackball** (turned by the gyro and every other camera move: for each of its axes, Yaw (Y, up: turning left and right), Pitch (X, right) and Roll (Z, along the laser's beam, through the screen at rest), you pick which gyro turn turns it (Yaw / Pitch / Roll / None) and can invert it; **None** holds that axis at 0 whatever the others do, as Camera never rolls (saved Lock flags from older versions read as None). **Turns about**: **The sphere's axes** (default) turns it as a gimbal, left and right about the sphere's vertical, then up and down about its own right, then about where it faces (`GyroPose.gimbalTurn`, `OverlayPose.GimbalTurn`), and each row has **Stop at ±90°**: Pitch (on by default) stops at straight up or down (±89°, the poles), and without it goes over the top with left and right staying as seen; Yaw and Roll (off by default) stop once turned sideways. **Its own axes** turns it about its own axes as it now points (left and right about its own up, up and down about its own right, `GyroPose.turnOwn`, `OverlayPose.Turn`), so the crosshair moves at right angles as it sees it and never rolls by itself; there are no poles, so Stop at ±90° is hidden. Moving in a loop leaves it a little rolled (the sphere's curvature: a loop's twist equals its area), so picking it also turns on Roll's ease back When the phone stops, which levels that out. By default it turns as Camera does with the default gyro mouse: the gyro's yaw turns it left and right, pitch up and down (stopping at the poles), Roll None, about the sphere's axes, and **As the camera** puts that back); Camera and Trackball turn as far as the game's camera (when its turn is known) or Spin 0.1–10× times the phone, have no Gravity, Compass or Turn amount, and Level sets them straight; a Trackball has no overall Ease back: instead each axis row has **Ease <axis> axis back** with its own options (On ratchet or When the phone stops, Delay, By time or By speed; default off), easing just that axis to straight (`GyroDisplay.trackballEase`, `OverlaySettings.TrackballEase`). **Level** (the way the phone is held now shows face-on; with Gravity only the tilt is kept), **Gravity** (tilt held to gravity, default on), **Compass** (heading held by the magnetometer, off), **Ease** back to straight (on): after 300 ms still, over 2 s or at a speed (90 °/s); **Ease on ratchet** (on: while a ratchet is held it eases back until straight, instead of whenever the phone stops); heading only (without gravity). **Amount**: **Turn amount** (0.5–3×, not on a trackball, which turns by Spin) and **Level**.

The phone's own gyro displays use the same Look and Turning settings, names and defaults (see [Pad UI](phone-pad-ui.md#gyro-displays)); their values are separate.

**Save as preset** (above Remove) saves the picked overlay as a preset named by its label (else its kind's name): every setting and the window's size, not its place (`HostSettings.OverlayPresets`). Presets show under **Add overlay** after the kinds with a ★; clicking one opens a new window from it where a new overlay opens; right-click one to **Rename…** or **Delete** it. The phone's presets are separate.

An overlay's label names it in the list, the tray menu's **Show** items, the Remove question and the caption at its bottom edge while Edit is on; the overlay never draws it otherwise.

**Shortcuts** (global, `Hotkeys`, a hidden message window so they work with no overlay open; reconfigurable, one press never does two things): **Ctrl+Alt+R** Level all, **Ctrl+Alt+O** Hide all, **Ctrl+Alt+E** Edit. A **Set orientation** mapping on the phone (or its Set orientation button) starts every overlay shown again from the phone's new base orientation: its saved Level grip cleared, facing ahead, Fade when phone rests counting from the way the phone is held then (OVERLAY byte 139 counts its presses; a change starts them over, the first count seen in a session does nothing). In the phone's World and Player gyro space (OVERLAY flag 128) overlays show the phone's real pose against gravity and take no saved grip. A **Clear displays** mapping clears every laser pointer overlay the same way (OVERLAY byte 148).

**Fade when phone rests** (a card under the shortcuts, for every overlay; `OverlayRest`, `HostSettings.OverlayRestFade`; on by default): an overlay fades while the phone lies on a table. It rests for an overlay once, for the whole **Quiet for** time (3 s, 0.5–30 s), the phone has lain within **Flat within** (10°, 1–45°) of the tilt that overlay was last leveled at (Level, Level all or Set orientation save which way was up, `OverlaySettings.LevelUp`, with or without Gravity; an overlay leveled before this reads it from its resting grip, one never leveled never fades), turned slower than **Still below** (0.5 °/s, 0.1–5, measured per OVERLAY; the card shows the phone's speed now, "still" or "moving"), read about 1 g on the accelerometer, and had no finger, pressed control, ratchet, stick off centre or attached controller's button, trigger or stick in use (a latched control is a state and doesn't count). A frame without gyro counts as moving, so a held phone (which shakes at 1–3 °/s) never rests. It then fades to **Fade to** (30 %) of its own opacity over **Fade over** (0.5 s) and comes back at once on any input or movement. **Also when the phone stops sending** (on): no OVERLAY for the quiet time (screen off, app closed, link stopped) fades them too. Never in Edit. A swap-chain window only changes its constant alpha (free); a copied one draws again.

## Edit and click-through

Overlay windows (`OverlayForm`) are borderless, always on top, per-pixel-alpha layered windows that never take focus. The mouse passes through them, except in **Edit** (one switch for all): then they can be dragged and resized by their edges, the mouse wheel resizes the picked window anywhere on screen (over another window it picks that one first), keeping its shape and centre (10 % a notch, 2 % with **Shift** held, never below 120×90; a low-level mouse hook while Edit is on, so it works with Windows' "Scroll inactive windows" off, and the wheel then reaches no other window except the companion's own, which still scroll; the hook runs on its own thread with its own message loop, so the PC's mouse never waits on the companion's UI thread, and only the wheel resizes go to the UI thread), and a click without a drag picks a window, cycling through overlapping ones. A double-click opens the companion window on the Overlays page with that window picked, or brings it to the front if it is already open (`TrayApp.OverlayClicked`). Each has an owner window rather than the tool-window style, so **OBS Window Capture** can record it, over games running windowed or borderless.

## Drawing

- **GPU**: one shared Direct3D 11 / Direct2D / DirectWrite device (`D2DCanvas`), presented by a swap chain without a CPU copy, with at most one picture per swap chain waiting for the graphics card (the device's frame latency set to the number of swap chains, 1–3; not 1 for the device, as the windows present one after another and each would wait for the one before), so on a busy graphics card an overlay drops behind by a frame at most instead of three; a lost device (sleep, driver update) is replaced. When making a swap chain fails, the windows copy their pictures through the CPU (UpdateLayeredWindow) for a while and then try it again: 2 s after a failure, 5 minutes after 3 in a row, one window at a time once its copied picture has drawn, the others following once it works. **CPU fallback** (GDI) when Direct2D can't start.
- **Pacer** (`OverlayPacer`): a background thread waits for each compositor refresh (DwmFlush), spaced by at least the refresh period, and draws there, not on the UI thread, also in Edit (moving a window needs no new picture). The UI thread draws a window only right after its size changed (its swap chain is resized there, at most once a frame however fast an edge is dragged), and a copied window in Edit (its picture would race the move and resize there); a settings change (a slider step) is drawn by the pacer at its next tick, whatever the window's frame cap. It sleeps while no overlay is open. Each window draws at most at its frame rate. The windows read the engine's published overlay view and the PC controller's own copy, never the engine lock, so drawing can't hold up input ([threads](pc-companion.md#threads-and-timing)). The pacer ticks at the fastest screen's refresh rate among the screens the open windows are on (each window reads its own screen's rate every 2 s), every Nth refresh for the fastest window's cap, sleeping through the refreshes it skips and still waiting for the compositor (DwmFlush) before the one it draws at.
- **Only what changed**: a window draws again when a new phone frame shows something new: what it draws besides the pose (fingers, presses, latches, sticks, layout, flags, the controller) differs from the last picture, or the pose turned 0.25 px or more at the window's size (its longer side, times the turn amount), so a still phone's gyro noise draws nothing. Also when the layout, trails, the laser pointer's trail, paint, fire or aim, Edit or the settings change, and every 500 ms for the status. Faded out by Fade when phone rests, it draws at most 4 times a second, until the rest ends.
- Draw times are logged every 30 s. In game the overlays cost about a fifth of one CPU core.
