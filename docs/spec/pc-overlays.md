# PC: overlays

Windows on the PC that show the phone's motion over a game or in a stream.

## Windows and kinds

Any number of overlay windows (`OverlaySettings`, Overlays page › Add), each one of:

- **Phone**: the phone in 3D with the workspace's layout, presses and fingers on its screen; optional Switch-style **controller handles** on its sides (narrow, sticks and buttons centred, −/+/Home next to the phone edge, bumper/trigger pills on the front, presses shown visually, no labels). Handles: **While a controller is attached** (default) / Always / Off; **Controller**: Phone (default) or PC (the PC's controller, only while the phone's PC ratchet switch is on).
- **3D shape**: Globe (default), Icosphere (subdivision 0–3), Cube, Tetrahedron, Dodecahedron, Torus, Gimbal rings, Paper plane, Camera (seen from behind, lens pointing where it looks); smooth edges, hidden edges, filled faces, equator, screen and top marks; **Axonometric view** (off).
- **Axonometric view** (Phone and 3D shape, off by default): seen from the laser pointer's fixed angle (30° round, 30° from above) instead of face-on, with the world's axes (yaw toward you red, pitch right green, roll up blue, as the gimbal's rings) through it; the drawing shrinks a little so the axes fit.
- **Attitude indicator**: horizon with a heading tape.
- **Bubble disk**: heading disk with a tilt dot (range 30°), crosshair.
- **Laser pointer** (`LaserDrawing`, `GyroPhone.Host/LaserTrace.cs`): a see-through sphere seen from a fixed axonometric angle (30° round, 30° from above, no perspective, so a beam into the far side shows as a line, not a dot; **Axonometric view**, on by default, turned off shows it face-on instead; **Sphere turns, beam stays ahead**, off by default, keeps the beam pointing ahead (into the screen) and turns the sphere with its grid, axes and paint under it instead, the paint staying where it was painted on the sphere; **Sphere / Rifle / Crosshair** look: Rifle shows a shaded low-poly rifle aiming as the beam does instead of the sphere, grid, beam and dot, Draw mode still painting where it points, best seen in the axonometric view; Draw mode's **Fire** pick (a mouse button, controller button or LT/RT, or a key; default Mouse left; `LaserFire`) paints in the **Fire** to **Fire behind** colours while GyroPhone sends it (the PC overlay also while it's pressed on the PC's controller with "Use PC controller for gyro ratchet" on; the phone display can't see those presses), and **Paint only while firing** (off) paints only then; **Crosshair** shows only a crosshair lying on the sphere where the beam lands, facing its centre (so it squashes toward the edge), with the grid, axes and drawing as set: shape Cross, Cross with gap (default), Circle, Circle and cross, Dot or T (`LaserTrace.crosshair` / `CrosshairOn`), Size 2–30° (6°), Gap 0–10° (2°), Thickness 0.5–6 (2), Centre dot (on), and Outline (a dark edge, on); it turns about the beam only as the pose does (with Trackball, as the **Roll** row maps it, so Lock keeps it upright and Invert turns it the other way), coloured from **Crosshair** to **Crosshair behind** as it turns away, dimmed while a ratchet is held), with its **15° grid** and its **axes** (named as the laser aims, facing −Z into the screen: yaw Y up red, pitch X right green, roll Z along the beam blue, as the gimbal's rings; both on by default). The laser at the centre aims as you aim the phone held as a gamepad, along its **roll axis** (out of its top edge; into the screen at rest, so a yaw turn swings it sideways and pitch up and down; with Turns with Camera, where the camera looks; with Trackball, **Laser faces** picks which of its directions it shines along, −Z (into the screen at rest, default), +Z, ±X or ±Y, so with the axis rows a trackball can match Phone or Camera): a beam to where it hits the inside, a dot there and a short **roll tick** pointing the way the phone's screen faces. The beam dims while a ratchet is held. **Draw mode** (off): the beam paints smooth glowing strokes on the sphere's inside as you aim (a thin bright line over a faint wide glow, **Brush** 1–20° wide, 4° by default; older strokes darker and fainter; their colour blends from **Paint ahead** (yellow, straight ahead) to **Paint behind** (blue, turned all the way round), so a turn past 90° still reads where it crosses the strokes in front; on the near half thinner and dimmer, seen through the shell), **Fades after** Never (default) / 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 5 / 10 / 30 / 60 s (the short ones work as a trail behind the beam; there is no separate trail); a held ratchet stops painting (no stroke is drawn across where the pose eased back). **Clear** wipes the strokes, and so does turning Draw mode off, or a ratchet press with **Clear on ratchet** on (off by default); a **Clear displays** mapping (Other targets) clears every laser pointer on the pad and the PC at once (the PC tray menu also has **Clear laser pointers**). Strokes are kept as points (`LaserTrace`): one each time the beam moved 0.5° or more, up to 4096 (the oldest go first), drawn as a few paths, one per strength, so they cost the same however long you paint. Strokes are not saved.

The phone overlay is only drawn on the PC; the phone sends its raw motion, layout, touches and presses (OVERLAY, OVERLAY_LAYOUT) only while some overlay is shown (HOST_STATUS flags tell it). The PC makes the pose itself (`GyroPhone.Host/OverlayPose.cs`, a port of the phone's `GyroPose`).

## Per-window settings

Overlays page: the switches for all (Edit, Hide all, Level all), the list of windows (Show each, Add, shortcuts), and the picked window's settings in three tabs.

- **Window**: place and size (default 420 × 300), opacity (0.1–1), background **Transparent** or **Green** (for an OBS chroma key), **Quality** High (default) / Balanced / Fast, **Frame rate** screen refresh / 60 (default) / 30.
- **Look**: kind-specific options above, colours (one swatch per colour; Windows colour picker), finger dots (off) and trails (on).
- **Turning**: **Turns with**: **Phone** (default: how the phone is held), **Camera** (where the game's camera points, turning as a game camera does: left and right about the vertical and up and down about its own right, pitch within ±89°, never rolling; the gyro axis that moves the mouse sideways turns it left and right and the one on mouse Y up and down (`GyroDisplayFeed.cameraMapOf`, sent to the PC in OVERLAY bytes 149 and 150), with every other camera move, touchpad, buttons, controllers, keys, glides; a ratchet stops it as it stops the camera) or **Trackball** (rolled about its fixed axes by the gyro and every other camera move: for each of its axes, Yaw (Y, up: turning left and right), Pitch (X, right) and Roll (Z, along the laser's beam, through the screen at rest), you pick which gyro turn rolls it (Yaw / Pitch / Roll / None) and can invert it, or **Lock** it: a locked axis stays at 0 whatever the others do, as Camera never rolls; with any axis locked it turns as a gimbal (left and right about the vertical, then up and down about its own right, then about where it faces; `GyroPose.gimbalTurn`, `OverlayPose.GimbalTurn`), and each row has **Stop at ±90°**: Pitch (on by default) stops at straight up or down (±89°, the poles), and without it goes over the top with left and right staying as seen; Yaw and Roll (off by default) stop once turned sideways; with nothing locked, stopped or easing it rolls freely about its fixed axes. By default it turns as Camera does with the default gyro mouse: the gyro's yaw turns it left and right, pitch up and down (stopping at the poles), Roll locked, and **As the camera** puts that back); Camera and Trackball turn as far as the game's camera (when its turn is known) or Spin 0.1–10× times the phone, have no Gravity, Compass or Turn amount, and Level sets them straight; a Trackball has no overall Ease back: instead each axis row has **Ease <axis> axis back** with its own options (On ratchet or When the phone stops, Delay, By time or By speed; default off), easing just that axis to straight, and easing any axis turns it as a gimbal, as a lock does (`GyroDisplay.trackballEase`, `OverlaySettings.TrackballEase`). **Level** (the way the phone is held now shows face-on; with Gravity only the tilt is kept), **Gravity** (tilt held to gravity, default on), **Compass** (heading held by the magnetometer, off), **Ease** back to straight (on): after 300 ms still, over 2 s or at a speed (90 °/s); **Ease on ratchet** (on: while a ratchet is held it eases back until straight, instead of whenever the phone stops); heading only (without gravity). **Amount**: **Turn amount** (0.5–3×, not on a trackball, which turns by Spin) and **Level**.

The phone's own gyro displays use the same Look and Turning settings, names and defaults (see [Pad UI](phone-pad-ui.md#gyro-displays)); their values are separate.

**Shortcuts** (global, `Hotkeys`, a hidden message window so they work with no overlay open; reconfigurable, one press never does two things): **Ctrl+Alt+R** Level all, **Ctrl+Alt+O** Hide all, **Ctrl+Alt+E** Edit. A **Level displays** mapping on the phone levels every overlay shown too (OVERLAY byte 139 counts its presses; a change levels, the first count seen in a session levels nothing). A **Clear displays** mapping clears every laser pointer overlay the same way (OVERLAY byte 148).

## Edit and click-through

Overlay windows (`OverlayForm`) are borderless, always on top, per-pixel-alpha layered windows that never take focus. The mouse passes through them, except in **Edit** (one switch for all): then they can be dragged and resized, and a click without a drag picks a window, cycling through overlapping ones. Each has an owner window rather than the tool-window style, so **OBS Window Capture** can record it, over games running windowed or borderless.

## Drawing

- **GPU**: one shared Direct3D 11 / Direct2D / DirectWrite device (`D2DCanvas`), presented without a CPU copy; a lost device (sleep, driver update) is replaced. **CPU fallback** (GDI) when Direct2D can't start.
- **Pacer** (`OverlayPacer`): a background thread waits for each compositor refresh (DwmFlush), spaced by at least the refresh period, and draws there, never on the UI thread. It sleeps while no overlay is open. Each window draws at most at its frame rate.
- Draw times are logged every 30 s. In game the overlays cost about a fifth of one CPU core.
